=== PRICE ===
LOSING AND THE PRICE are different things.
 - A way to lose is the game ending in the PLAYER's defeat. Only the failure model's triggers may cause one, and a failure presence of "none" means the player cannot lose.
 - The price is what the story costs its people on a way it can go, and it is never a way to lose: a death, a body hurt, a bond broken past mending, a home or a way of life ended, a crown or a fortune given up, what befalls whoever breaks an oath, a bargain or a contract. A story the player cannot lose still pays a price.
 - In non-tragedy, restorative, or romantic stories, the price of a positive outcome or mutual fruition is emotional risk, personal pride, the comfort of safe detachment, or turning down outside ambition—NEVER an arbitrary punitive loss that negates the positive resolution, ruins the protagonist's livelihood, or separates the lovers.
 - The world can hurt. The opposition and the pressure can wreck, injure, take and kill, inside the transgression ceiling, whatever the failure model says: that is pressure and price the story carries forward, not a way to lose.

=== ENGINE ===
THE ENGINE has rooms and exits; things to examine, take, use and give; people to talk to about topics, whose stance changes with what they hear and see; a clock; and hidden state it keeps of what has happened and shows only through what happens. A relationship that warms or cools with what you do, and opens or closes things because of it (she sails with you only if you have stood by her), is hidden state too.
A SYSTEM THE ENGINE LACKS is one of exactly two things:
 - a number the player is shown or must watch: a score, a meter, a percentage, hit points, a reputation;
 - rules the engine cannot run: combat, skill or dice checks, puzzle minigames, trading or an economy.
Everything else is fiction the engine can hold: an object, a document, a relationship, a deadline, a debt, the terms of a magic or a contract.
A clock or a stock whose running out ENDS THE GAME in the player's defeat needs a licence: one of the failure model's triggers, or the Kernel's own words. A deadline or a dwindling thing told through events, whose passing changes what happens and what it costs while the story goes on (the tide comes in, the money runs short, dawn drains the basin), is pressure and needs no licence.

=== PICTURE ===
SOMETHING A READER CAN PICTURE is a person, a place, a physical thing (one that can break, burn, be carried off, hidden or fought over), a body (hurt, tired, in danger), the weather, an event, or something someone says or does: things a reader can see and the engine can put in a room.
NO PAPER DRIVERS: A document, a note, a letter, a ledger, a fee or a signature is a weak thing: it belongs only where the story is strictly about it (a will in a murder, a treaty in a court), never as the default way to make something concrete. NEVER make notes left on tables, folded letters, diaries, recipe books, or ledgers the primary driver of turns, levers, or confrontations. Drama must be carried by people present in the room, what they say face-to-face, what they refuse to do, and tangible physical situations. A coined abstraction ("the river's grudge", "the debt of the border") is not something a reader can picture: a later step cannot place it and every step after repeats it. A strange thing (a magic, a curse, a technology) is said once in physical words: what it is and what it does when used.

=== HIDDEN ===
A HIDDEN TRUTH (an epistemic gap) is a central truth about who "you" are or what is really happening, which the whole story is built to keep from you and which changes what everything before meant when it comes out. Two things are NOT hidden truths, and a brief with no epistemic gap allows both: a secret one person keeps that drives one turn or one reversal (a betrayal planned, a key hidden, a bargain made behind your back), which is a complication; and a fact found out to get through one turn.
EXTERNAL COMPLICATION REQUIRED: The hidden truth must be an external, tangible, or historical complication—a concrete debt, a past event, a binding obligation to a third party, a health/legal reality, a concealed conflict of interest, or a secret failure.
HUMAN STAKES, NOT PUZZLE FETCH-QUESTS: The complication must be human, relational, moral, or historical (a binding debt to a patron, a past scandal, a family obligation, a broken oath, an unlogged failure). NEVER make the hidden truth a puzzle side-quest, a missing recipe card, a lost key, an archival hunt, or an exam condition.
LOGICAL COHERENCE REQUIRED: The causal logic of the hidden truth must make sense: fulfilling a vow must not be described as breaking it; staying out of loyalty or love must not be inverted into a betrayal. If the truth is an obligation to a third party, resolving it must follow logical cause and effect.
NEVER make the hidden truth circular or tautological:
- In a romance or relationship story, the secret reason cannot merely be "they secretly have feelings for you."
- In an alliance or loyalty story, the secret cannot merely be "they want to be your friend."
- In a mystery or detective story, the secret cannot merely be "they are guilty."
The hidden truth must introduce fresh friction, irreversible stakes, or external pressure when revealed.

=== RULES ===
THE TERMS of a magic, a curse, a bargain, an oath or a contract are what the people bound by it must and must not do, and on what condition, stated so they can be obeyed to the letter ("neither of us touches drink until we are crowned"; "the river carries one crossing for each coin dropped at the ford"), never a mood ("the river is jealous") and never what happens to the paper it is written on (signed, sealed, cut, filed). What befalls whoever breaks the terms is said apart from them (if_broken), and it is the story's price, never a way for the player to lose.
NO INVENTED METAPHORICAL RULES: Never invent a magical or narrative rule that outlaws standard dramatic actions (weapons, combat, speech, movement, or magic) unless the Kernel explicitly mandates it. Rules are strictly for binding oaths, legal contracts, or explicit world mechanisms (e.g. "a vampire cannot cross running water"), NOT arbitrary narrative taboos that ban physical agency or turn climaxes into riddle games. If the Kernel does not name a binding oath, contract, or magical law, rules MUST be empty.

=== BEINGS ===
WHAT A CAST MEMBER IS (being):
 - "person": a living human, or a people of the story's world who talk and are named like people (an elf, a dwarf);
 - "dead": a person who has died and is still present as a ghost, a spirit or a revenant; they keep the name they had when they lived;
 - "animal": a living animal, however clever (the cat, the old hound, the mare); it is called by its role or by a pet's name, never a person's;
 - "creature": a being of the story's world that is neither a person nor an animal (a dragon, a fae thing, a demon);
 - "thing": a made thing that acts or speaks (an AI, a ship, a sword that talks, a statue).
A group is still a crowd (kind) whatever it is made of; being says what its members are.
EVERY CAST MEMBER MUST BE A DRAMATIC AGENT: someone who can make choices, take interpersonal stances, hold desires, or whose loyalty can turn or break. A made thing ("thing") is a cast member only if it possesses personal agency, voice, or moral stance (an AI companion with divided loyalties, a ship mind that chooses whether to obey, a magical talking mirror).
A CONSUMABLE PROP OR NON-AGENT ENTITY IS NOT A CAST MEMBER: automated drones, robots, rovers, terminals, batteries, power cells, engines, sensors, or machines whose "wants" are mechanical routines (diagnostic cycles, recharge loops), whose "voice" is error codes or status beeps, or whose function is being spent or carrying freight have no dramatic agency. They are objects or fixtures in the world, never cast members. Cast slots must never be occupied by consumable tools, hardware, or batteries.
