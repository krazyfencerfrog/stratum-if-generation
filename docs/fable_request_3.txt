This goal of this project is to take generate story content for the use in an interactive fiction engine.  It takes a user's minimal idea for a story (the kernel) and expand its it with not additional user involvement into a full story for use with the engine.

take a look at docs/fable_request_2.txt to see the previous request that you fulfilled.

you created corrective phases for 3.5 and 3.75, and got step 4 into a working state.  however we have a number of issues remaining that we need to resolve.

I ran kernel1 for many many hours and got through two iterations of step 4. The output should be in stories/current_output.tar.  additional test story kernels are in tests/kernels.

*) You added optimization lines to all the prompts to try to limit the reasoning traces.  They looked like great additions but didn't seem to affect the overall length of the reasoning traces.

*) The story design is weak. I think the main problem is that the choices don't feel earned.  For example, in kernel1, you jump from vent/spare to vent/spare to vent/spare ... to end.  That's not an engaging story.  What is your motivation on this path?  To cut the minimum sectors while protecting the overall integrity?  What is the obsticle?  Wouldn't it be better if you the AI did *not* have the power to vent/spare, but had to convince the sector representatives to make the decision (majority wins)?  The story is then about interacting/manipulating the representatives to make the 'right' choice.  Note I'm not saying use this idea, I'm asking why we didn't come up with a more complex idea then vent/spare...

*) Step 4a is doing too much.  Why is it concerned with plotting out other endings?  The idea of 4a is to make a path through the story.  Iterations of 4a then identify a different kind of path through the story and make a branch point (or fullfill an existing branch point) to make it work.  Consider the original star wars movie.  The original plot is the first path through the story. Luke's motivation is something like a simple teenage desire for escape and adventure to a profound sense of duty and vengeance after losing his family.  The second iteration should try to find another motivation and the work is to modify the graph in such a way that we have a good explanation on how/why the motivation shifts.  for example let's say mid story an opportunity arises that luke is in contact with darth, who explains (early) that he is his real father, and he was stolen away as a child, possibly negating the motivation of losing his foster family.  This could present an early choice for luke to accept darth mid-way through the story, and work with him covertly to infiltrate the rebellion.  It doesn't have to be just motivation, it can also be a change of strategy or something else.  It just needs more punch and less work.  Feel free to split this into more pieces, the real hard line for me is processing time and context size for a single prompt (generally measurable by the size of the reasoning trace).

*) I'm wondering if we're giving too much freedom to the kernel to determine story shape + ending counts.  The hard specification of 6-7 endings is really giving the generation a coniption, and I'm thinking the shape shouldn't necessarily be up for discussion either.  What do you think about this?  Is it reasonable for the kernel to drive this, or should we filter it out in step2 and let the rest of the process figure out what's best?  I'm honestly not sure what the right answer is here.

*) A major omission from me is the exact kind of interactive fiction we're trying to make here.  It appears you've been proceeding under the idea of a twine-like choose-your-own-adventure style options.  Instead, our engine is a room-based exploration/interaction engine like INFORM7 / TADS / ADRIFT, though with a different interface system to avoid word guessing problems.  So every node in the story really represents a section of the story where the user can explore a subset of rooms, interact with characters/objects, and the result of that interaction/clock will be the driving force of what path gets taken in terms of a branch (and affects long term state).  I wanted to keep the engine details out of the outline process, but that was likely a mistake as we are definitely seeing assumptions about option-based traversal in the output already and we probably need to think about the room / character subsets early.

*) The generation seems to like to make unspecific groups of people as characters, likely to keep what seems like an insanely harsh limit satisfied.  I think having a 'crowd' as a 'character' is an option, but it should always have a representative character (or multiple) to interact with.
