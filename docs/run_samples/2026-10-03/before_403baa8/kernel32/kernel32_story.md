# kernel32: story outline

Kernel: A dungeon crawl where your only companion is a cursed talking sword that wants to be put back where it was found. Funny, but with teeth.

## Story form: Fichtean curve + In medias res
The story begins immediately in the flooded reliquary with the sword already pointing at the exit, escalating through a series of tightening hazards and wrong turns where the sword's desire to return becomes a physical threat. It should land on the final escape, where the bond is resolved in a wry, bittersweet restoration rather than a homecoming or a total loss, matching the 'funny, but with teeth' tone.
Why this form: Its shape of opening inside the first crisis and climbing through distinct crises to a climax matches the 3b.primary_trajectory of 'escalating' and the 3e.timeline_duration of 'hours_to_days'. The 'Aftermath' beat accommodates the 3c.moral_valence of 'wry, bittersweet restoration' after the 3-0c.failure_triggers of 'hazard_or_wrong_turn' have played out, whereas save_the_cat's 'Finale' requires a mirroring of the opening image which is less suited to the linear, single-footprint setting of 3f.setting_structure.

The question: Does the sword stay with you as a companion, or go back to where it belongs?

## Lines
### T1: The Dripping Niche
- motivation: You start out wanting to get out of the reliquary alive with the cursed sword still in your hand, because it is the only companion and weapon in the flooded chamber. By the end you want the water and the tightening chamber to stop, even if that means letting the sword go back to the place it has been pointing at all along.
- strategy: Use the sword's warnings and cuts while promising the return niche, and keep the sword in hand until the reliquary makes the return impossible to postpone.
- turning point: In the third crisis, when the curse takes your hand and points it at the return niche, the sword's menace makes clear that keeping it in hand keeps you in the water; from then on you stop treating the return as a later debt and let the final escape force the setting down.
- ending (N05) The Calm Niche: The reliquary empties because the sword is set in its return niche, and you climb out alone with the water no longer chasing you. It cost your only companion and the easy certainty of its warnings, leaving only a faint voice in the niche and a dry step that belongs to you alone.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05

### T2: The Unletting Hand
- motivation: You start out wanting to get out of the reliquary alive with the cursed sword still in your hand, and by the climax you want the sword kept in your hand even if the water follows you, because an empty hand is the one price you no longer want to pay.
- strategy: Use the sword's warnings and cuts, promise the return niche when it buys the latch, and at the final choice refuse the niche and let the sword point the dry step.
- turning point: The sword points the dry step while the water closes in, and you choose the pointing over the niche's silence.
- leaves T1 after N04 when: you kept the cursed sword in your hand and let it point the dry step
  - instead of: you set the cursed sword in the return niche and let it calm the water
  - why the shift: The water has made the niche the only calm place, but the sword offers the only dry step; when the water moves to take that step, you stop wanting to be saved from the sword and start wanting to keep the one thing that can still show you where to stand.
- differs: T1 answers the question with belonging and pays with the lost companion, while this line answers with companionship and pays with a followed, unletting curse.
- ending (T2N01) The Kept Sword: You get out with the cursed sword still in your hand, and the water follows you to the threshold because the niche was left empty. You have kept your only companion and weapon, but you have not been let go: the sword's pointing hand does not release you, and the reliquary's wet certainty travels with you.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > T2N01

### T3: The Spent Warning
- motivation: You start out wanting to get out of the reliquary alive with the cursed sword still in your hand, and you come to want the way open without giving the niche its promise, because the promise is the first leash; by the end that want becomes wanting to keep the sword without letting it keep you, even if that means losing its warning.
- strategy: Force the sealed way open with the broken stair’s iron while the sword refuses to cut, keep the sword in your hand, and take the route the reliquary leaves unmarked rather than the route its warning would have bought.
- turning point: The iron prys the latch open and the sword’s refusal proves that the way can be forced instead of bought, so the promise stops being a debt and becomes a leash.
- leaves T1 after N02 when: you pried the latch with the broken stair’s iron while the cursed sword refused to cut
  - instead of: you promised the return niche and let it cut the latch open
  - why the shift: The sword offers its cut only for the promise, and the iron works anyway; you learn that the reliquary can be forced open without the niche’s bargain, and the promise you almost made becomes the first thing you do not want to carry.
- differs: Against T1, it keeps the sword and pays not in the sword’s absence but in the loss of its warning. Against T2, it keeps the sword without the bond closing into a keeping hand, because the unmarked wrong turn spends the sword’s guidance.
- ending (T3N03) The Unmarked Step: You get out with the cursed sword still in your hand and the water still at the threshold, but the sword’s warning is spent and the wrong turn is left unmarked. The price is the guided route: the sword stays with you as a weapon, not a companion, and every step after the threshold is yours to take without its pointing.
- path: N01 > N02 > T3N01 > T3N02 > T3N03

## Nodes
### N01: The Wrong Turn [Opening crisis; turn 1, way 1]
lines: T1, T2, T3
where: the flooded reliquary chamber
who: the cursed sword

The flooded reliquary chamber is full of black water, and the cursed sword points first at the return niche, then laughs when you step toward the dry alcove that is only the niche's shadow. You let it warn you and take the broken stair before the water finds the trap. The water rises a hand's breadth behind you, and you reach the broken stair still carrying the thing that wants to be put back.

### N02: The Promised Latch [Second crisis; turn 2, way 1]
lines: T1, T2, T3
where: the flooded reliquary chamber
who: the cursed sword

At the sealed way out, the cursed sword says it will cut the wet iron latch only if you promise the return niche, and you promise it while your pack drips through the water. You let it cut the latch open, and the way out stands open with the promise hanging in the water. The water begins draining toward the niche, making the niche the easiest place to reach and the hardest to leave.

### N03: The Pointed Hand [Third crisis; turn 3, way 1]
lines: T1, T2
where: the flooded reliquary chamber
who: the cursed sword

The curse takes your hand and points it at the return niche while the water surges to claim the niche. You let the cursed sword finish pointing, wrench your hand back, and step past it with the sword still in your grip. The sword knows you will not set it down, its warning turns to menace, and the reliquary tightens around the open way out.

### N04: The Setting Down [Climax; turn 4, way 1]
lines: T1, T2
where: the flooded reliquary chamber
who: the cursed sword

The water is closing the chamber, and the cursed sword makes its desire to be returned plain one last time. You set it in the return niche and let it calm the water enough for the open stair to stand dry. You leave alone, and the niche keeps the last of its voice, leaving your hand empty and the way out clear.

### N05: The Dry Step [Aftermath; ENDING]
lines: T1
where: the flooded reliquary chamber
who: -

Beyond the threshold, the reliquary is quiet and the water no longer follows you, while your empty hand is the only thing left pointing. You stand with a lighter pack and a colder certainty that the niche now holds what it was found to hold. The sword's company is gone, leaving only a faint voice behind and a dry step that belongs to you alone.

### T2N01: The Kept Sword [Aftermath; ENDING]
lines: T2
where: the threshold beyond the reliquary
who: the cursed sword

Beyond the threshold the water is still at your heels, and the cursed sword's pointing hand has not let go; the niche's silence is behind you. You get out with the cursed sword still in your hand, and the water follows you because the niche was left empty. The crawl is over, but the bond is not restored; it is kept, and it keeps you, naming the next step as you leave.

### T3N01: The Pointed Hand [Third crisis; turn 3, way 1]
lines: T3
where: the flooded reliquary chamber
who: the cursed sword

The curse takes your hand and points it at the return niche while the water surges toward the unmarked wrong turn the sword did not warn you about. You let the sword finish pointing and wrench your hand back, and it knows you will not set it down, so its warning turns to menace over the hole you left unmarked.

### T3N02: The Forced Latch [Climax; turn 4, way 2]
lines: T3
where: the flooded reliquary chamber, the threshold beyond the reliquary
who: the cursed sword

With the water closing the chamber, the sword gives its desire one last time while you pry the latch open with the broken stair's iron and it refuses to cut. You keep it in hand and let it point the dry step, and the step holds as the water follows you to the threshold. The sword's pointing hand does not let go of the unmarked wrong turn, which is now the only thing it will name.

### T3N03: The Spent Warning [Aftermath; ENDING]
lines: T3
where: the threshold beyond the reliquary
who: the cursed sword

Beyond the threshold the water is still at your heels and the sword is still in your hand, but its warning is spent and the wrong turn remains an unmarked hole in the reliquary. You have kept the weapon and lost the guide, so the bond is only a thing that points at what it cannot fix. Every step after the threshold is yours to take without its pointing.
