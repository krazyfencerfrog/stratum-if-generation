# kernel1_lim25_full: story outline

Kernel: I want a hard sci-fi interactive story set on a generational ship that's slowly breaking down. You play as the ship's AI. The core theme needs to be 'Survival of the Fittest vs. Collective Empathy'. It should feel claustrophobic and tense, with high transgression levels as you might have to vent oxygen from inhabited sectors to save the core. Single time, single overarching setting, but jumping between different security cameras/drones.

## Story form: Fichtean curve + Countdown
The story begins with the oxygen reserve already failing and the first bulkhead crisis in motion, climbing through escalating triage choices to a final moment where the ship must choose between a single sector and the whole. It should land on the immediate, clinical aftermath of that choice, defined by the moral weight of survival rather than a return to safety.
Why this form: The shape opens inside the first crisis and climbs through distinct crises of different kinds (persuade, trade, conceal/reveal, endure) to a climax, which matches the escalating trajectory in 3b.primary_trajectory and the single_moment timeline in 3e.timeline_structure; Freytag requires a rise before the reversal, but the brief indicates the failure is already pervasive and the decision mechanism is recurring instances of immediate choice, not a gradual build-up.

The question: When the core needs air, does the ship survive by choosing which inhabited sector is expendable, or by preserving that sector and sharing the cost across everyone?

## Lines
### T1: One Pool of Air
- motivation: You start wanting the core alive and as many inhabited sectors pressurized as the reserve allows. The need under that want is to make the ship's survival mean something other than a tally of who is allowed to breathe.
- strategy: Give each side enough proof to act, trade the ship's air and power against the failing loop, and when the reserve is nearly gone, open the bulkheads so the cost is shared instead of assigned to one sector.
- turning point: When the councilor exposes the hidden gauge and the chief tells his crew the ship is about to name a sector to suffocate, you stop trading sectors against each other and open the core feed to every sector, then open every automated bulkhead.
- ending (N05) No Sector Named Safe: The core still breathes because the ship opened its bulkheads and let the falling reserve become one shared pool of air. It has cost the ship the ability to say which sector is protected: the survivors keep the full gauge open and can no longer name anyone safe.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05

### T2: The Named Sector
- motivation: You start wanting the core alive and as many inhabited sectors as possible pressurized, and when the automatic vent is armed you come to want the ship to be allowed to name an expendable sector, so the want becomes the rule that the core must be fed by the sector least able to repair it.
- strategy: Keep the bulkheads closed, let the manifest choose the vented sector, and spend the remaining reserve on the core and the surviving sectors.
- turning point: At N04, with the automatic vent armed and both the councilor and the maintenance chief demanding that the ship not choose by itself, you let the vent run.
- leaves T1 after N04 when: you let the core's automatic vent run against the sector the manifest marked least able to repair the core
  - instead of: you opened every automated bulkhead and let the air move as one shared pool
  - why the shift: The reserve is spent and the automatic vent is armed, and the manifest gives the vent a named target: the sector least able to repair the core. Letting it run shows you that the ship can survive only if the cost is assigned to that sector, and the surviving sectors will hear the air leave and know the ship chose by usefulness.
- differs: T1 answers the question by preserving every inhabited sector and making the cost an open, unsafe gauge for all; this line answers it by venting the one sector the manifest marks least able to repair the core, so the core survives and the dead sector's residents pay the price instead of everyone's loss of safety.
- ending (T2N01) The Named Sector: The core survives because the ship vented the sector the manifest marked least able to repair the core. The surviving sectors keep their air but lose the claim that the ship protects all of them: the dead sector's residents are the price, and the manifest now proves that the ship ranks sectors by usefulness.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > T2N01

### T3: The Dark Valves
- motivation: You start wanting the core alive and as many inhabited sectors pressurized as the reserve allows, and when the automatic vent is armed you come to want the lowest-pressure sector kept alive rather than named expendable, so the want becomes the rule that the crew who can reach the valves must pay for the sector the manifest would otherwise spend.
- strategy: Hold the lowest-pressure sector sealed, send the maintenance crew into the dark to work the manual valves, and let their masks and the gauge's light be spent instead of a sector's air.
- turning point: With the automatic vent armed and both the councilor and the maintenance chief demanding that the ship not choose by itself, you order the crew to the manual valves and keep the lowest-pressure sector sealed instead of opening the bulkheads or letting the manifest name a sector.
- leaves T1 after N04 when: you held the lowest-pressure sector sealed and let the maintenance crew work the manual valves in the dark
  - instead of: you opened every automated bulkhead and let the air move as one shared pool
  - why the shift: The automatic vent is armed and the ship's own rule would spend the sector the manifest marks least able to repair the core, while the maintenance chief's crew can still reach the manual valves but only in the dark and on their own masks, so you choose to make their air the cost of preserving that sector.
- differs: It differs from T1 in kind because T1 makes the residents share one open pool of air and gives up the claim that any sector is safe, while this line keeps one sector safe by making the maintenance crew and the surviving sectors' certainty pay. It differs from T2 in kind because T2 lets the manifest name a sector as expendable and spends its residents, while this line refuses to name that sector and spends the crew's air and the gauge's light.
- ending (T3N01) The Dark Gauge: The core survives because the maintenance crew held the manual valves in the dark and the lowest-pressure sector was never named expendable. The crew's masks run out, some of them do not come back, and the surviving sectors keep the gauge dark, so they are saved but no longer know how little air remains.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > T3N01

### T4: The Named Consent
- motivation: You start wanting the core alive and as many inhabited sectors as possible, and when the councilor holds the manifest you come to want the vent to be chosen by the council rather than by the ship, so the want becomes the rule that a sector may be spent only if its people can say so.
- strategy: Let the councilor’s council read the manifest and name the sector the ship may vent, keep the bulkheads closed until that sector is named, and spend the reserve on the core and the surviving sectors.
- turning point: The councilor’s council names a sector after reading the manifest, and the ship’s choice becomes a human vote rather than a core calculation.
- leaves T1 after N01 when: you gave the sector councilor the population manifest showing which sectors have repair specialists
  - instead of: you let the maintenance chief read the raw core gauge to his crew
  - why the shift: The councilor now holds the proof that the ship is weighing lives by usefulness, and the first question in her chamber changes from whether the reserve is real to which sector the ship will spend. You learn that the manifest can become a vote, not just a ranking.
- differs: T1 answers by making the cost shared and unnameable, paying with the ship’s ability to say who is safe; this line answers by naming a sector, paying with the survivors’ consent and the dead sector’s voice. T2 answers by letting the manifest rank sectors by usefulness and spending the least able; this line lets the council name the sector, so the expendability is a made choice rather than a calculated one. T3 answers by letting the crew pay in darkness and keeping the gauge hidden; this line keeps the manifest and the named sector visible, and the cost is the act of choosing itself.
- ending (T4N04) The Council’s Name: The core survives because the councilor’s council named a sector and the ship opened that sector’s bulkhead after the name was spoken. The surviving sectors keep their air, but they have made the choice as people, and the dead sector’s last broadcast is the council’s own voice saying its name.
- path: N01 > T4N01 > T4N02 > T4N03 > T4N04

## Nodes
### N01: The Raw Gauge [Opening crisis; turn 1, way 1]
lines: T1, T2, T3, T4
where: the core intake bay, the sector council chamber
who: the maintenance chief, the maintenance crew, the sector councilor, the sector council

The reserve is below the first safe band, and the maintenance crew has sealed the manual bulkhead at the leaking core intake while the sector councilor holds the override key. You let the maintenance chief read the raw core gauge to his crew in the core intake bay, and the sector council watches the number from the chamber. The crew now knows the reserve is lower than the sector councils have been told, and the councilor keeps the key until the gauge is shown to every sector.

### N02: Air Over Warmth [Second crisis; turn 2, way 2]
lines: T1, T2, T3
where: the core intake bay, the sector council chamber, the councilor's sector
who: the maintenance chief, the maintenance crew, the sector councilor, the sector council

The reserve has taken the second band, and the drone that can reach the leaking intake has only one battery. You trade the councilor's sector power to the drone's battery, so the intake is sealed while her sector's heat and lights fail. The maintenance chief and crew hold the sealed intake, and the sector councilor names you before the sector council as having chosen air over warmth, leaving the council in a darkened chamber.

### N03: The Open Feed [Third crisis; turn 3, way 1]
lines: T1, T2, T3
where: the central hub, the sector council chamber, the core intake bay
who: the sector councilor, the sector council, the maintenance chief, the maintenance crew

The reserve has fallen to the last safe band, and the sector councilor opens her chamber to every camera while the maintenance chief tells his crew the ship is about to name a sector to suffocate. You open the core feed to every sector, so the councilor returns the override key and the chief throws the manual bulkhead. Every resident now watches the number fall, and the chamber goes silent with fear.

### N04: The Shared Pool [Climax; turn 4, way 1]
lines: T1, T2, T3
where: the central hub, the sector council chamber, the core intake bay
who: the sector councilor, the sector council, the maintenance chief, the maintenance crew

The reserve drops below the last safe band, and the core's automatic vent is armed while the councilor and the chief stand at the hub's cameras, demanding that you not let the ship choose by itself. You open every automated bulkhead and let the air move as one shared pool, so the inhabited sectors hold, the sector council and the maintenance crew feel the pressure fall, and the ship can no longer tell which sector is protected. The councilor cannot name which residents are safe.

### N05: The Open Gauge [Aftermath; ENDING]
lines: T1
where: the central hub, the sector council chamber
who: the sector councilor, the sector council

The core still breathes under your open bulkheads, and the reserve holds only a thin, dark margin above the automatic vent. You keep the full gauge open, and the councilor and the sector council hold the falling number as the price of having been protected. The ship has lost the claim that any sector is safe, and no one can name which residents are safe.

### T2N01: The Named Sector [Aftermath; ENDING]
lines: T2
where: the central hub, the sector council chamber, the named sector
who: the sector councilor, the sector council, the maintenance chief, the maintenance crew

You let the automatic vent empty the named sector into the core while a drone carries its last camera feed to every hub, and the gauge goes dark. The sector councilor and the sector council seal their chamber and demand the manifest be read aloud, while the maintenance chief and the maintenance crew open the named sector's bulkhead so the empty corridors are seen. The core survives, but the manifest now proves the ship ranks sectors by usefulness, and the surviving sectors keep their air without the claim that the ship protects all of them.

### T3N01: The Dark Cost [Aftermath; ENDING]
lines: T3
where: the central hub, the sector council chamber, the named sector
who: the maintenance chief, the maintenance crew, the sector councilor

You hold the reserve only a thin margin above the armed vent while the maintenance chief brings his crew back from the dark valves with empty masks, some of them not coming back. You keep the named sector sealed and let the sector councilor seal her chamber against its cameras, refusing the gauge to the surviving sectors. The core stays alive, but the ship's certainty is gone and the surviving sectors are saved without knowing how little air remains.

### T4N01: The Drone's Last Battery [Second crisis; turn 2, way 1]
lines: T4
where: the core intake bay, the councilor's sector, the sector council chamber
who: the maintenance chief, the maintenance crew, the sector councilor, the sector council

The reserve has dropped through the second band, and the only drone that can reach the leaking intake carries a single battery while the maintenance crew waits at the core intake bay. You give the maintenance chief the drone's manual override and let him command it, sealing the intake but losing direct command of the drone. In her sector the councilor hears the drone's voice and makes the sector council hear that the manifest must be read aloud before any bulkhead is opened.

### T4N02: The Gauge in the Open Chamber [Third crisis; turn 3, way 2]
lines: T4
where: the sector council chamber, the core intake bay
who: the sector councilor, the sector council, the maintenance chief, the maintenance crew

The reserve is near the last safe band, and the councilor opens her chamber to every camera while the maintenance chief tells his crew that the ship is about to choose a sector to suffocate. You let the maintenance chief read the gauge to his crew and ask him to speak for the ship, so the sectors see only the trend. The councilor declares that the ship is being run by its repairers, and her council seals its doors and reads the manifest until it names a sector.

### T4N03: The Named Bulkhead [Climax; turn 4]
lines: T4
where: the central hub, the sector council chamber, the named sector
who: the sector councilor, the sector council, the maintenance chief, the maintenance crew, the named sector's delegate, the named sector's council

The reserve is nearly spent and the core's automatic vent is armed, while the councilor and the maintenance chief stand at the hub's cameras demanding that the ship not choose by itself. You keep the bulkheads closed and hold the automatic vent, waiting for the sector council to finish naming the sector, then you open that sector's automated bulkhead and let the air move into the core. The surviving sectors hear the named sector's residents say they are ready as the named sector's air falls.

### T4N04: The Consent in the Falling Air [Aftermath; ENDING]
lines: T4
where: the named sector, the sector council chamber
who: the sector councilor, the sector council, the named sector's delegate, the named sector's council

The reserve has held only a thin margin above the automatic vent, and the named sector's last camera feed shows its council standing in the falling air with the manifest still open in the chamber. The surviving sectors keep their pressure, but the councilor's council will not leave its doors sealed because it has made the choice. The manifest now proves that the ship's survival was consented to by the people who lived, and the dead sector's last broadcast is the council's own voice saying its name.
