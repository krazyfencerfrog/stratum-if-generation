# kernel28: story outline

Kernel: A mystery. Nobody dies and there is no crime.

## Story form: Kishotenketsu + Episodic middle
The story moves through a sustained, low-stakes investigation where the protagonist gathers clues from different sources, building to a gentle revelation that reconciles the mystery with the shop's reality. It should land on a satisfying, peaceful resolution that resolves the epistemic gap without dramatic conflict or high-stakes confrontation.
Why this form: Its shape of introduction, development, turn, and reconciliation matches the brief's sustained trajectory (3b.primary_trajectory) and light tone (3b.tone), where the 'turn' is the revelation of the hidden truth (3-0b.epistemic_gap) rather than a conflict, and the 'reconciliation' aligns with the best case of a gentle resolution (3c.moral_valence).

The question: What is the marked loaf for, and what will you let it mean?

## Lines
### T1: The Marked Loaf
- motivation: You start out wanting to find out who is leaving the marked loaf and why, because the owner will decide by the end of the day what the display case becomes. By the end you want the owner to be able to choose what the loaf means, not just be given an answer that would leave the case painted plain and the reason unasked.
- strategy: Follow the human lead quietly: use the note, the regular’s cup, and the old order slips to bring the regular’s reason to the owner before the buyer leaves, without turning the morning into a story.
- turning point: The back office. Once the old order slips show that the mark belongs to an old recipe and that the head baker has been bringing the note to the owner each morning, you stop trying to prove what the mark is and start letting the owner choose what it means.
- ending (N05) A Plain Counter With a Marked Loaf: The shop reopens as a plain takeaway counter, but with the marked loaf still in the case and the regular’s note left on the counter where the owner can see it. It has cost the morning’s quiet, the regular’s privacy, and your promise to keep the regulars calm, but the owner has chosen the sale and let the small ritual stay.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05

### T2: The Stuck Lock
- motivation: After the owner warns you off the back office, you come to want the buyer’s afternoon kept clear of a mystery, and then to want the ordinary explanation to be true enough that no one has to be asked why the loaf is there.
- strategy: Follow the physical lead: show the owner that the case lock was stuck and the loaf could have come from inside, keep the regular’s note out of the decision, and let the ordinary explanation stand.
- turning point: When the owner accepts the stuck-lock account and keeps the back office closed, the mystery stops being about the regular and becomes about the case.
- leaves T1 after N03 when: you showed the owner the case lock was stuck and that the loaf could have come from inside
  - instead of: you showed the owner the note and let the regular speak first
  - N03 must now contain: Add to N03 that the changed lock’s catch is stiff and can be sprung from inside, so you can point to it as the ordinary cause.
  - why the shift: The owner’s warning and the buyer’s afternoon make a personal story look like a complication, and the stuck lock gives you a way to protect the sale without asking the regular; you start wanting the mystery to stay inside the case.
- differs: T1 brings the regular’s reason to the owner and leaves the ritual in the case; this line lets the ordinary cause stand and the person who left the loaf is not asked, so the price is the unasked reason and the thinness of the answer.
- ending (T2N02) The Plain Counter: The owner signs for the plain takeaway counter with the supplier’s delivery note beside the stuck catch, and the marked loaf is explained as an old supplier mark. The regular’s reason is not asked, the note stays out of the decision, and the morning ends with the sale kept clear and the personal meaning left resting.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > T2N01 > T2N02

### T3: The Old Habit
- motivation: You start wanting to find out who is leaving the marked loaf and why, but once the head baker answers without naming the person, you come to want the morning kept from becoming a story while still giving her account a place in the owner's decision.
- strategy: Follow the head baker's lead: let her old-recipe explanation stand, keep the regular's identity out of the owner's choice, and present the mark as an old habit rather than a complaint or a message.
- turning point: The head baker will say the mark is old but will not say who left it. Once you accept that partial answer as the one the owner needs, the investigation becomes about what you let the loaf mean instead of who put it there.
- leaves T1 after N02 when: you showed the head baker the note and let her answer in her own time
  - instead of: you followed the regular's cup to the regular who marked the loaf
  - why the shift: The head baker takes the note, says the mark is old, and does not name the person, while the owner hears that you have been asking. The answer is enough to stop the morning from becoming a hunt for a culprit, but it leaves the regular's reason unasked, so you begin to want the owner to hear the head baker's account rather than the regular's.
- differs: Compared with T1, the regular's reason is never spoken aloud and the owner accepts an old habit rather than preserving a visible ritual. Compared with T2, the explanation is human and deliberately left unfinished rather than settled by a physical supplier fact.
- ending (T3N03) The Unnamed Loaf: The owner signs for the plain takeaway counter and leaves the marked loaf in the case as an old habit rather than a message. The head baker's silence is the price: the regular's reason is not asked, the note is not read aloud, and the morning ends with a true but unfinished explanation that keeps the shop calm and the buyer's afternoon clear.
- path: N01 > N02 > T3N01 > T3N02 > T3N03
