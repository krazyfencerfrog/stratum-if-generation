# kernel17: story outline

Kernel: A noir detective story. depending on how the case plays out.

## Story form: Fichtean curve + In medias res
The story opens in the immediate aftermath of a death, already under the pressure of a pre-determined cover-up, and moves through a series of escalating, time-compressed crises as the detective fights to keep the truth alive against institutional inertia. It lands on a decisive confrontation where the case is either formally closed over the truth or left as a compromised, hard-won justice, with little time for reflection afterwards.
Why this form: The brief specifies a tight timeline (3e.timeline_duration) and a sustained trajectory (3b.primary_trajectory) driven by recurring instances of decision-making (3-0a.decision_mechanism) under irreversible foreclosure (3-0c.failure_triggers). The Fichtean curve's structure of opening in crisis and climbing through distinct crises matches the engine's four turns (discover, persuade, trade, confront) which are all active struggles against the pressure to close the file, rather than a setup-and-complication arc. The short aftermath aligns with the 'hours_to_days' span and the noir tone of 3b.tone, where the resolution is the immediate procedural outcome.

The question: Which lead or suspect do you press, and does the case become a conviction or a protection?

## Lines
### T1: The Key in the Locker
- motivation: You start out wanting the killer before the precinct closes the case over the dock thug. By the end you want the captain to name the clerk even if it exposes the pension fund and leaves your career marked, because the case must stop being a protection.
- strategy: Press the lead that exposes the guilty party, trade only what the captain can take, and put the clerk before the lawyer at morning roll.
- turning point: The moment you take the ledger from the captain's locker instead of asking for it; from then on the case is against you as well as the clerk.
- ending (N05) A Name, Not a File: The captain names the commission clerk at morning roll, and the commission's pension fund is exposed. You keep the truth, but the survivor is moved, the clerk is gone, and the precinct marks you as the detective who opened the file.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05

It becomes a wish to keep the commission safe and the drawer sealed, even if the truth stays buried.
- strategy: Trade the confession for the captain's closure and let the commission lawyer take the ledger, so the case stays on the dock thug and the drawer is sealed.
- turning point: The captain offers to close the case on the dock thug's confession if you let the lawyer take the ledger; once you hand over the confession, the case becomes a protection instead of a conviction.
- leaves T1 after N04 when: you took the ledger from the captain's locker, kept the survivor's signed statement, and handed the dock thug's confession to the captain while the commission lawyer took the ledger (accumulated)
  - instead of: you laid the ledger and the survivor's signed statement on the captain's desk
  - why the shift: The survivor is already in a private ward, the captain has seen you with the ledger, and the commission lawyer is waiting; the captain offers to close the case on the dock thug's confession if you let him keep the ledger, so the only thing left to protect is your career and the precinct.
- differs: T1 presses the clerk and makes the case a conviction that exposes the pension fund at your career's cost, while this line presses the dock thug and makes the case a protection that keeps the commission safe and seals the drawer, paying the price with the buried truth and the victim's file.
- ending (T2N01) The Closed File: At morning roll the captain closes the case as a robbery gone wrong by the dock thug, the commission lawyer takes the ledger into the commission's offices, and the victim's drawer is sealed. You keep your career and the precinct safe, but the truth stays buried, the clerk is never named, and the case becomes a hollow win.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > T2N01

### T3: The Conviction
- motivation: You come to want a conviction more than you want the clerk named, because the captain will only close the case on a man the commission can afford to convict and the survivor's signed statement is the only thing that can make the dock thug stand as the guilty party.
- strategy: Let the captain take the dock thug's confession at morning roll and leave the survivor's signed statement in his hand so the case closes on the dock thug as a conviction rather than a sealed file.
- turning point: At morning roll the captain offers to take the confession and name the dock thug, and you see that the survivor's signed statement can carry the case without opening the commission's files.
- leaves T1 after N04 when: you brought the survivor's signed statement and the dock thug's confession to morning roll and let the captain take the confession (accumulated)
  - instead of: you laid the ledger and the survivor's signed statement on the captain's desk
  - why the shift: The captain offers to close the case on the dock thug if you let him take the confession, and the survivor's signed statement is already in his hand, so the conviction can be made without exposing the commission's files or naming the clerk.
- differs: T1 exposes the clerk and leaves you marked, while this line convicts the dock thug and leaves the clerk's lead unopened. T2 seals the truth as a hollow win, while this line makes a real conviction but spends the survivor's statement and locks the office.
- ending (T3N01) The Dock Thug's Name: The captain names the dock thug at morning roll, the commission lawyer accepts the confession, and the survivor's signed statement is read in his favor. The office door is locked, the clerk is never named, and you are left unmarked, but the case becomes a conviction that the survivor and the locked office carry.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > T3N01

### T4: The Marked Protection
- motivation: You come to want the case closed as a robbery gone wrong because the captain has already seen you with the ledger and the survivor has been moved, and a sealed file is the only thing that can keep the commission from opening the clerk's lead while you carry the mark. By the end you want the file to record you as the one who moved it, so the protection does not become a clean lie you can walk away from.
- strategy: Give the captain the dock thug's confession for the commission's signature, hand the ledger to the commission lawyer, and sign the evidence log yourself so the file names you as the detective who moved it.
- turning point: At morning roll you no longer ask the captain to name the clerk. You ask him to close the case, and you make the file carry the cost of that closure in your own name.
- leaves T1 after N04 when: you had taken the ledger from the captain's locker, kept the survivor's signed statement out of the room, and brought the ledger and the dock thug's confession to morning roll with the captain and the commission lawyer waiting (accumulated)
  - instead of: you laid the ledger and the survivor's signed statement on the captain's desk and let him name the commission clerk
  - why the shift: The captain has already seen you with the ledger, and the survivor is already moved, so the clerk can no longer be named without exposing the commission's own file. The only way to keep the case from opening further is to let it close as a robbery gone wrong and let the evidence log carry the mark instead of the captain's name.
- differs: T1 presses the clerk and pays with the pension fund exposed and your career marked; this line presses the protection and pays with the truth sealed and your career marked. T2 gets the same sealed protection but leaves you unmarked; this line takes the same sealed protection and makes the mark yours. T3 lets the dock thug stand as the conviction and leaves the survivor carrying the case; this line keeps the case a protection and leaves you carrying the record of it.
- ending (T4N01) The Evidence Log: At morning roll the captain takes the dock thug's confession, the commission lawyer takes the ledger, and the case is closed as a robbery gone wrong. The clerk's lead is crossed out and the victim's drawer is sealed. You sign the evidence log as the detective who moved the ledger, so the file protects the commission and marks you at the same time. The truth stays buried, the dock thug is left without a real case, and you carry the mark of the case you closed.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > T4N01
