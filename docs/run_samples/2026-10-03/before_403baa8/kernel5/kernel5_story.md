# kernel5: story outline

Kernel: A messy family drama set during a chaotic weekend wedding at a big estate. You hop between the viewpoints of three different siblings. The stakes are purely emotional—secrets coming out, old grudges, inheritance fights. It should be funny but have moments of real gut-punch sadness.

## Story form: Three-act structure + Relay
The story moves through a single weekend, escalating from the arrival of the inheritance documents to their public reading at the reception. It should land on the moment of the reading itself, where the secrets are forced into the open, resulting in a bittersweet reconciliation that leaves the family changed but not shattered.
Why this form: The story has a clear inciting incident (the papers arrive) and a decisive confrontation (the reading at the reception) which aligns with the three_act structure's climax; save_the_cat requires a 'false victory' and 'all is lost' low point that contradicts the sustained trajectory and lack of failure presence in the brief, while kishotenketsu lacks the distinct crisis beat that the 'gut-punch' sadness and confrontation require.

The question: Which of the family's secrets, grudges, or inheritance claims do you surface, and which do you keep in?

## Lines
### T1: The Reading at the Reception
- motivation: You start out wanting to get through the wedding weekend with the family still a family and the estate's future honestly settled. By the end you want the truth to be heard in the room, even if it costs a clean signature and a private apology.
- strategy: Use the executor's papers, seating, rooms, and signatures to make each sibling's claim count where it can be answered, surfacing what must be said and carrying what still must be kept.
- turning point: The executor's signature book shows the eldest sibling's line was signed before the rehearsal dinner, so the claim can no longer be managed privately; the only way left is to let the papers be read where everyone can hear them.
- ending (N07) A Family in the Room: The inheritance papers are read aloud at the reception, the parent's sealed letter is no longer private, and the eldest sibling's pre-signed line turns a grudge into a claim that must be answered. The estate is left unsettled but shared, and the family is changed, not shattered, because the truth was heard before the weekend ended.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05 > N06 > N07

### T2: The Private Signing
- motivation: You come to want the estate closed legally before the reception becomes a trial, and that want becomes the need to keep the letter and papers out of the room even if the siblings' claims are not heard.
- strategy: Keep the sealed letter out of the room and the inheritance papers off the table, trading a public reading for a private signing.
- turning point: At the reception, the sealed letter on the table and the heirloom in your hand make a public reading feel like a verdict, so you trade the reading for a private signing.
- leaves T1 after N06 when: you gave the executor the sealed letter and asked for a private room
  - instead of: you handed the executor the family heirloom as proof of the claim
  - N06 must now contain: The executor must be shown able to table the inheritance papers and move the signing to a private room when the sealed letter is offered.
  - why the shift: The sealed letter is already on the table and the heirloom is in your hand, so a public reading would turn grief into a verdict, and the executor's willingness to table the papers makes legal closure possible before the guests force an answer.
- differs: T1 answers the question by surfacing the papers and letter in the room and leaving the estate unsettled but shared, while this line answers by keeping the claim and apology in and closing the estate legally, with the eldest sibling paying the cost of believing the family chose silence.
- ending (T2N01) Closed in Private: The estate is legally settled in a private room, the family is kept together, and the reception ends without the papers being read aloud, but the eldest sibling's claim and the middle sibling's apology are kept in, and the eldest sibling leaves believing the family chose silence over the future.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05 > N06 > T2N01

### T3: The Closed Estate
- motivation: You come to want the eldest sibling’s pre-dated claim answered before the weekend ends, and that want becomes the need to get the estate legally closed even if the middle sibling’s claim and apology are not heard.
- strategy: Put the signature book in the eldest sibling’s hand, let them sign in front of the guests, and keep the middle sibling’s claim and apology out of the signing.
- turning point: The executor makes clear that the estate cannot be reopened after the ceremony, so the signature book becomes the only way to answer the pre-dated line before the reception ends.
- leaves T1 after N06 when: you placed the executor’s signature book in the eldest sibling’s hand
  - instead of: you handed the executor the family heirloom as proof of the claim or gave the executor the sealed letter and asked for a private room
  - N06 must now contain: Add to N06 the executor’s statement that the estate cannot be reopened after the ceremony, so the signature book must be used before the reception ends.
  - why the shift: The executor tells you the signature book is the only way to close the estate before the weekend ends, and the middle sibling’s claim is not part of the papers being signed.
- differs: T1 surfaces the middle sibling’s truth and the eldest sibling’s grudge in the room and leaves the estate unsettled; this line surfaces only the eldest sibling’s claim by making them sign and leaves the estate closed. T2 keeps the eldest sibling’s claim and the middle sibling’s apology out of the room and leaves the eldest sibling believing in silence; this line brings the eldest sibling’s claim into the room and closes the estate by signing over the middle sibling’s voice.
- ending (T3N01) The Closed Estate: The eldest sibling signs the inheritance papers in front of the guests, the executor closes the estate over the pre-dated line, and the middle sibling’s claim and apology are kept out of the signing. The estate is legally settled, but the middle sibling is signed over without their voice, and the youngest sibling’s apology is lost in the signing.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05 > N06 > T3N01

### T4: The Apology as Closure
- motivation: You start out wanting the eldest sibling's private apology to be heard before the estate is closed, and that want becomes the need to let that public apology buy a legally settled estate even if the claim itself is kept out of the room.
- strategy: Let the executor read the first line of the sealed letter aloud, then use the seating and signature book to move the inheritance papers into a private signing so the claim is answered legally rather than publicly at the reception.
- turning point: Once the executor reads the first line aloud, the apology is no longer private, and the estate's closure can be built around that public admission instead of the pre-dated claim.
- leaves T1 after N02 when: you let the executor read the first line of the sealed letter aloud
  - instead of: you heard the letter from the middle sibling, who had already opened the envelope in the hallway
  - N02 must now contain: The executor takes the sealed letter from the table and reads the first line aloud before the middle sibling can explain having opened the envelope.
  - why the shift: The first line makes the eldest sibling's private apology audible in the room, so the apology can no longer be carried privately into the estate's settlement. The executor immediately treats the letter as a record that can be used to close the estate without a public reading of the papers.
- differs: T1 surfaces the claims at the reception and leaves the estate unsettled; this line surfaces the apology early and keeps the claim out of the reception, leaving the estate legally closed at the price of the apology becoming public. T2 keeps both the letter and the papers in and leaves the apology unsaid; this line trades the apology's privacy for legal closure. T3 surfaces the eldest sibling's claim by putting the signature book in their hand; this line keeps the claim in and surfaces the apology instead.
- ending (T4N05) The Closed Apology: The inheritance papers are kept out of the reception and the estate is legally closed in a private signing over the eldest sibling's pre-dated line. The eldest sibling's apology has been heard, but the claim itself is kept in, and the middle sibling's claim and apology are left unanswered in the room. The family is legally settled, but the price is that the apology now belongs to the estate's record rather than to the family's private life.
- path: N01 > N02 > T4N01 > T4N02 > T4N03 > T4N04 > T4N05
