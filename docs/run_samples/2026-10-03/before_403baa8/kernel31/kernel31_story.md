# kernel31: story outline

Kernel: An epic fantasy adventure. You're a young hedge-witch from a border village who gets swept up in a quest to stop an old dragon that has woken in the northern mountains. Along the way you gather a small party — a disgraced knight, a thief who knows the old roads, and a scholar from the capital who has her own reasons for wanting to reach the dragon first. I want real travel: forests, a river city, ruins, the mountain pass. The companions should matter, with their own loyalties that can pull against yours. different endings depending on how you deal with the dragon and who is still with you at the end.

## Story form: Hero's journey (condensed)
The story begins in the hedge-witch's border village, a known world disrupted by the dragon's waking, and moves through a dispersed journey of escalating wonder and loyalty tension. It lands on the confrontation at the pass, where the protagonist must resolve the threat while preserving the bonds of the party, rather than returning home or unwinding into loss.
Why this form: Its shape matches the dispersed setting (3f.setting_structure) and the linear, weeks-long journey (3e.timeline_duration) where the protagonist leaves a known world and is remade by the ordeal. Unlike freytag or three_act, its final beats are the road back and return, which allows for the 'bonds preserved' ending (3c.moral_valence) to be felt as a change in the protagonist's relationship to the party, rather than a simple resolution of the threat.

The question: How do you stop the awakened dragon, and which bond is spent to do it: the old duty of the quest, or the loyalty of the companions who have walked with you?

## Lines
### T1: The Border Charm
- motivation: You start out wanting to stop the dragon before it reaches the border villages, and by the end you want to stop it without becoming the kind of person who spends the people who walked with you.
- strategy: Follow the old road, trade for what each companion holds, and keep the border charm in your own hand as long as you can.
- turning point: At the foot of the pass the scholar makes the old name depend on one carrier, and you pay the thief with your border charm to keep the road open. Once the charm is in his keeping, the old name is no longer a method you control; it is a test of whether he will stand.
- ending (N08) A Scarred Pass: The dragon passes through the pass as a hedge-witch's wake, spared by the companions who still stand with you, but the scholar turns away to settle her family's claim alone and the wake is left partly unresolved over the border villages. You return home with the knight and the thief, and with a road that no longer belongs only to the capital.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05 > N06 > N07 > N08

### T2: The Capital's Mark
- motivation: You start out wanting to stop the dragon before it reaches the border villages, and by the pass you come to want the wake sealed for good, because the charm alone only carries it and leaves the old road open.
- strategy: Present the scholar's old name with the knight's oath and the river city's ward-stone, letting the capital's claim bind the dragon to the old road.
- turning point: At the pass, you set aside the border charm and give the scholar's old name the key to the wake, so the quest is answered by the old road instead of by the companions who stand with you.
- leaves T1 after N06 when: you presented the scholar's old name with the knight's oath and the river city's ward-stone
  - instead of: you refused the old name and let your border charm carry the wake through the companions who still stand with you
  - why the shift: The wake is already over the first fields, and the border charm only slows it; the scholar offers the old name from the capital letter as the seal, and the knight's oath and the ward-stone are the only things there that can make it hold.
- differs: T1 answers with the companions' loyalty and leaves the wake unresolved under a hedge-witch's charm; this line answers with the old duty of the quest and leaves the dragon bound under the capital's mark, so the scholar's claim, not the scholar's presence, is spent.
- ending (T2N02) The Bound Road: You come back to the border village with the knight and the thief, and with the old road sealed under the capital's mark. The dragon is bound to the pass and the villages are safe, but the scholar's claim is settled against the village as the first debtor, and the road now answers to the capital's letter rather than to your charm.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05 > N06 > T2N01 > T2N02

### T3: The Unanswered Wake
- motivation: You start out wanting the wake settled without making the scholar carry her family's claim alone, and by the pass you come to want the party to survive the choosing; that want becomes, too late, the wish that someone else had been forced to pay the price instead of you.
- strategy: At the pass, let the scholar withhold the old name and let the companions who doubted you turn away, so the wake passes over the border villages under the weight of the broken party.
- turning point: The scholar says the old name will make the village accept the capital's debt, and you do not make her speak it; the quest's duty then turns into the companions' withdrawal, and the wake is no longer answered by any bond you have kept.
- leaves T1 after N06 when: you let the scholar withhold the old name and let the companions who doubted you turn away
  - instead of: you refused the old name and let the thief's border charm carry the wake through the companions who still stood
  - N06 must now contain: Add to N06 that, when the wake rises, the scholar says the old name can be spoken only if the village accepts the capital's mark, and that she will not speak it alone; the knight, whose broken oath you have not mended, and the thief, whose road now depends on a charm no one will carry, turn away when you do not force her to speak.
  - why the shift: You have watched the old name turn the scholar's claim, the knight's oath, and the thief's road into debts, and at the pass you understand that making her speak it would spend the village as the first debtor. You let her withhold it, and the companions who doubted you read that as your refusal to choose them.
- differs: T1 answers the question by spending the scholar's claim and leaving the wake partly unresolved, but the knight and thief still stand with you; this line answers it by spending the companions' loyalty itself, so the wake passes over the villages and the party breaks. T2 answers the question by binding the dragon under the capital's mark and making the village pay the debt; this line refuses both the old name and the charm, leaving the debt unsettled and the villages exposed.
- ending (T3N02) The Unsettled Wake: The dragon's wake passes over the border villages without being sealed or carried through, and the villages are left with thinned wards and widening frost-cracks. The scholar leaves with the capital letter to settle her family's claim and the village's debt by her own method, the knight and the thief go their separate ways, and you return alone to the border village with no charm carried into the wake and no road answered.

### T4: The Scholar's Settlement
- motivation: You start out wanting to stop the dragon without losing the companions, and by the foot of the pass you come to want the village spared from the capital's debt, so the scholar's family claim becomes the thing you must let be spent.
- strategy: Let the scholar carry her capital letter into the wake and settle her family's claim in person, so the old name binds the dragon while the village is not made the first debtor.
- turning point: At the foot of the pass you give the scholar the chance to carry the name herself, and from then on the binding depends on her personal claim rather than on the village's wards or your border charm.
- leaves T1 after N05 when: you let the scholar carry the old name into the wake to settle her family's claim in person
  - instead of: you paid the thief with your border charm so that he carried the name into the wake
  - why the shift: The scholar says the capital letter will answer only if the claim is settled by the person who carries it, and you realize the village's wards are too thin to survive being named the first debtor.
- differs: It differs from T1 by answering the dragon through the old name and the capital's letter rather than through the hedge-witch's charm, so the wake is sealed instead of left partly unresolved. It differs from T2 by spending the scholar's personal family claim instead of the village's first-debtor debt, so the capital's mark remains but the village is not made to pay. It differs from T3 by keeping the dragon's wake resolved and the companions present at the pass, rather than letting the party fall apart and the wake pass over the villages unanswered.
- ending (T4N03) The Settled Claim: The dragon is bound to the old road under the capital's mark, and the scholar's family claim is settled against her rather than against the village. You return to the border village with the knight and the thief, while the scholar leaves with the spent letter and no remaining claim on the dragon's name; the villages are safe, but the road now answers to the capital's mark and the scholar's personal reason is gone.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05 > T4N01 > T4N02 > T4N03

## Nodes
### N01: The First Frost-Cracks [Ordinary world]
lines: T1, T2, T3, T4
where: the border village
who: -

In your border village you keep the small wards on the fields and read the first frost-cracks in the mountain ash that mark the dragon's waking. The village is short of hands and shorter of old magic, and your charm only holds the smoke away from the houses. The wake points north, and your charm is not enough to follow it.

### N02: The Capital Letter [The call]
lines: T1, T2, T3, T4
where: the border village
who: the scholar

The scholar arrives at the border village with a sealed letter and says the dragon's old name is buried in the northern ruins. You would rather stay and mend the village wards, but the letter names your village as the first debtor in her family's old claim. The scholar makes clear that if you do not walk, the capital will settle the debt without you, and you take the letter so the road north becomes a debt you must walk.

### N03: The Road-Mark [Crossing the threshold; turn 1, way 2]
lines: T1, T2, T3, T4
where: the ruined shrine
who: the scholar, the thief

At the ruined shrine the scholar finds the old name on the broken altar and says it can open the road to the pass. The thief says the old roads will not open for a name spoken by a capital woman. You read the old road-mark in the altar and learn the name can be carried by your border charm, while the thief keeps the road-mark for himself and asks to be paid at the river city, so the name passes from a capital letter into a test of who will carry it.

### N04: The Ward-Stone [Trials; turn 2, way 3]
lines: T1, T2, T3, T4
where: the river city, the city shrine, the market
who: the river city warden, the scholar, the thief, the knight

The scholar sets the route through the river city and says the old name must be carried with the city's ward-stone. The river city warden withholds the stone until someone can say who will answer the dragon, and the knight offers his broken oath to the city's shrine so the warden gives the stone. The thief presses for the payment he asked for at the shrine, and in the market the knight's disgrace is spoken while the scholar says the oath is not enough for the old name.

### N05: The Price of the Road [Trials; turn 3, way 3]
lines: T1, T2, T3, T4
where: the high pass
who: the scholar, the knight, the thief

At the foot of the pass the scholar says the old name will answer only if one of you carries her capital letter into the wake, while the knight will not carry it unless his oath is mended and the thief will not carry it unless his road is paid. You give the thief your border charm as the price of his road, and he takes the capital letter into the wake while your hedge-witch's charm passes into his keeping and the party's way north stands on his word.

### N06: The Wake at the Pass [The ordeal; turn 4, way 2]

The scholar's unsettled claim leaves her outside, and the wake passes through the pass only partly resolved.
