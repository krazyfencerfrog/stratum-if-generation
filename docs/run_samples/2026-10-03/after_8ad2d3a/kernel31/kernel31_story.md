# kernel31: story outline

Kernel: An epic fantasy adventure. You're a young hedge-witch from a border village who gets swept up in a quest to stop an old dragon that has woken in the northern mountains. Along the way you gather a small party — a disgraced knight, a thief who knows the old roads, and a scholar from the capital who has her own reasons for wanting to reach the dragon first. I want real travel: forests, a river city, ruins, the mountain pass. The companions should matter, with their own loyalties that can pull against yours. different endings depending on how you deal with the dragon and who is still with you at the end.

## Story form: Fichtean curve + In medias res
The story moves from a quiet border village into an escalating series of physical and relational crises, driven by the dragon's waking and the party's diverging loyalties. It builds to a single decisive confrontation at the mountain pass where the choice of how to stop the dragon determines who remains. The ending should feel like a resolution of the immediate threat that is inextricably tied to the state of the bonds formed along the way, landing on the specific composition of the party rather than just the dragon's fate.
Why this form: The brief's 3b.primary_trajectory is 'escalating' and the engine's 'turns' are a sequence of distinct crises (forest, city, shrine, pass) that climb to the final confrontation. Fichtean curve's shape of opening in crisis and climbing through crises of different kinds matches this structure, whereas Hero's Journey implies a return to an ordinary world which the brief's 3c.moral_valence (bonds preserved/broken) does not support as a primary landing point, and Three-Act is too generic for the specific 'crisis pile-up' defined by the engine.

The question: How do you stop the dragon: by the quest’s old force, or by the party’s shared cost?

## Lines
### T1: The Keeper's Debt
- motivation: You start out wanting to get the border villages out of the dragon's cold and prove that a hedge-witch can walk the old roads. By the end you want the party to live with what stopping the dragon costs, because saving the road without saving the people on it would leave you empty-handed.
- strategy: Travel north with the party, spend small witchcraft to read marks and keep them moving, and trade what you can carry for what each companion can give.
- turning point: At the ruined shrine, Corra Dunmore admits the ash in her vial is her mother's, and you learn the old binding is a debt, not a weapon; from then on the question is whether the party will stand in your ward while she pays it.
- ending (N05) The Sleep and the Forgetting: The old dragon sleeps under the pass, and Corra Dunmore wakes in the snow without knowing you, Rowan Hask, or Teague Barrowman; the party remains, but she is a stranger in it, and you have spent your ward to the bone.
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05

### T2: The Party's Strike
- motivation: You start out wanting to keep the party together through the dragon's waking, but once Corra Dunmore passes the gate and answers only to the order, you come to want the dragon driven out by what the party can still carry, because the old binding has already been taken out of shared hands.
- strategy: Keep the party behind the sealed gate, gather the oath-blade and the old road, and reach the pass before Corra Dunmore can complete the rite in her own name.
- turning point: At the ruined shrine you read the old rite from the stone and learn what the name will cost; Corra Dunmore says the rite is not for the party, and you understand that the party must meet the dragon as a threat, not as keepers.
- leaves T1 after N02 when: you let Corra Dunmore present the commission letter and pass the gate before the party
  - instead of: you gave Sorrel Ravel your warding smoke and he let the whole party on his boat
  - why the shift: Corra Dunmore passes through the order's gate and says she will answer only to the order, and the party is left on the far side of the sealed gate with the keeper no longer bound to travel with them.
- differs: T1 answers the question by letting the party stand in a ward while Corra Dunmore pays the binding and loses her memory of them; this line answers by driving the dragon out with the knight's blade and the thief's road, leaving Corra Dunmore behind at the broken pass as the keeper who could not finish the rite.
- ending (T2N03) The Broken Pass: The old dragon is driven out of the pass by the knight's oath-blade and the thief's old road, but its fire breaks the gate and Corra Dunmore is left behind as the keeper who could not finish the rite; the party survives, but the road north is open only at the cost of the one who carried the name.
- path: N01 > N02 > T2N01 > T2N02 > T2N03

### T3: The Shared Ward
- motivation: You start out wanting to keep the order’s record whole by following Corra Dunmore’s sealed route, but once the wake closes the road behind you and the vial remains unspoken, you come to want the dragon stopped by a cost the whole party can carry, because the old name is already too heavy for one keeper.
- strategy: Keep Corra Dunmore’s vial sealed and the party together, gather each companion’s mark of the road, and at the pass bind the name with a shared ward so the dragon sleeps without being driven out or leaving the keeper behind.
- turning point: At the ruined shrine the party learns the name will take a keeper’s memory, and you decide that the road’s cost must be divided before the pass.
- leaves T1 after N01 when: you let Corra Dunmore read the commission letter and led the party down her route before the wake closed behind you
  - instead of: you read the cold marks and took Rowan Hask's side road, and Teague Barrowman's oath-blade was left in the snow
  - why the shift: The wake closes the forest road behind you, so the party can no longer turn back, and Corra Dunmore keeps the vial sealed as the order’s only answer; you learn that the road has made the keeper’s burden the party’s burden, and you want a way to stop the dragon that does not cost another companion.
- differs: T1 stops the dragon by letting Corra Dunmore pay the name alone, leaving her a stranger in the party, while this line stops the dragon by dividing the name’s cost among the party and leaves Corra known to them but the road itself spent. T2 stops the dragon by driving it out and leaves Corra behind as the unfinished keeper, while this line stops it by the old name and leaves Corra with the party, but each companion loses a piece of the road they walked.
- ending (T3N04) The Road Spent: The old dragon sleeps under the pass, and Corra Dunmore still knows your name, Rowan Hask’s name, and Teague Barrowman’s name; the party remains, but each of you has paid a piece of the road, and the road north cannot be walked again as it was.
- path: N01 > T3N01 > T3N02 > T3N03 > T3N04

### T4: The Order's Keeper
- motivation: You start out wanting to keep the party together behind the sealed gate, but once Corra Dunmore passes the river city gate under the order's claim and says she will answer only to the order, you come to want the old binding finished in her own name, because the party's shared ward cannot be answered across a sealed claim.
- strategy: Follow Corra Dunmore behind the sealed gate, keep Rowan Hask and Teague Barrowman with you only as far as the order allows, and let her complete the rite alone at the pass.
- turning point: The gate seals after Corra Dunmore, and the party is no longer her keeper; from then on the road north is her claim, not the party's.
- leaves T3 after T3N01 when: you let Corra Dunmore present the commission letter and pass the gate first with the party behind her, and the gate sealed after her
  - instead of: you gave Sorrel Ravel your warding smoke and kept the party crossing as one group behind him
  - why the shift: The sealed gate is the first thing the order takes from the party: Corra Dunmore passes under the letter and leaves the party's claim behind, and you understand that if the rite is to happen at all, it will happen in her name, not in the party's shared ward.
- differs: T1 ends with the dragon sleeping and Corra Dunmore still in the party but not knowing them, while this line ends with her finished as the order's keeper and the party shut out of the rite. T2 ends with the dragon driven out and Corra Dunmore left behind unfinished, while this line ends with the dragon sleeping under the old name and the party excluded from the cost. T3 ends with the dragon sleeping under a shared ward that spends the road but keeps the party's names in Corra Dunmore's memory, while this line ends with the party unable to share the rite and the road north closed to them.
- ending (T4N03) The Sealed Rite: The old dragon sleeps under the pass, and Corra Dunmore stands at the altar stone as the finished keeper of the order, not knowing your name, Rowan Hask's name, or Teague Barrowman's name; the party remains outside the sealed pass, unable to share the rite or the road north, and the gate is closed behind them.
- path: N01 > T3N01 > T4N01 > T4N02 > T4N03

## Nodes
### N01: The Dead Side Road [Opening crisis; turn 1, way 1]
lines: T1, T2, T3, T4
where: the forest road
who: Rowan Hask (the thief), Corra Dunmore (the scholar), Teague Barrowman (the knight)

The forest road is ash-white around you, and the cold is coming down the trees while Corra Dunmore reads the capital's route from her commission letter and Rowan Hask points to a side road his map marks dead. You read the cold marks and take Rowan's side road, and falling ice cuts Teague Barrowman, leaving his oath-blade in the snow. Corra Dunmore keeps the vial sealed as the cold wake tightens behind you.

### N02: The Gate's Price [Second crisis; turn 2, way 1]
lines: T1, T2
where: the river city bridge
who: Corra Dunmore (the scholar), Sorrel Ravel (the ferryman), Ysolde Stannard (the gatekeeper)

At the river city bridge, Corra Dunmore shows Ysolde Stannard the commission letter, and Ysolde Stannard says the letter names her mother the broken keeper and orders the vial brought back unbroken, so the gate passes only the keeper, not her party. You give Sorrel Ravel your warding smoke, and he rows the whole party across the bridge while the cold you cannot bind for a day settles in your hands. Corra Dunmore sees that you spent your own power.

### N03: The Keeper's Ash [Third crisis; turn 3, way 1]
lines: T1
where: the ruined shrine
who: Corra Dunmore (the scholar), Teague Barrowman (the knight), Rowan Hask (the thief)

At the ruined shrine, the altar stone is split, and Corra Dunmore will not let you touch the ash in her vial. Teague Barrowman says the old binding was broken by a keeper, and Rowan Hask has found a keeper's mark on the crypt door. You show Corra Dunmore your ward and let her see that you read the ash; she tells you the ash is her mother's, but does not say what the true name will take, and you take the split altar stone with you for the pass.

### N04: The Pass and the Name [Climax; turn 4, way 2]
lines: T1
where: the mountain pass
who: Corra Dunmore (the scholar), Teague Barrowman (the knight), Rowan Hask (the thief)

The wake is already sealing the gate, and the old dragon waits in the cold beyond the pass. You give Corra Dunmore the altar stone and let her break the vial while Rowan Hask and Teague Barrowman stand with you in the ward you bind, and the old name takes her memory of everyone she met on the road. The dragon sleeps under the pass, but Corra Dunmore does not know you in the snow.

### N05: The Stranger in the Party [Aftermath; ENDING]
lines: T1
where: the mountain pass
who: Corra Dunmore (the scholar), Teague Barrowman (the knight), Rowan Hask (the thief)

The old dragon sleeps under the pass, and Corra Dunmore wakes in the snow holding the broken vial, not knowing you, Rowan Hask, or Teague Barrowman. Rowan Hask and Teague Barrowman stay with her because you will not leave her there, but the party you built on the road is gone from her. Your ward is spent to the bone, and she is a stranger in the party that remains.

### T2N01: The Crypt Rite [Third crisis; turn 3, way 3]
lines: T2
where: the ruined shrine, the shrine crypt
who: Corra Dunmore (the scholar), Teague Barrowman (the knight), Rowan Hask (the thief)

At the ruined shrine, Corra Dunmore is standing by the split altar stone with the vial sealed, and Rowan Hask has found a keeper's mark on the crypt door. You follow Rowan Hask into the shrine crypt and read the old rite on the stone, learning that the name will take the keeper's memory of everyone she met on the road. Corra Dunmore sees you pry and tells you the rite is not for the party, while Teague Barrowman brings back the oath-blade from the snow where it fell, cold-cracked but still his.

### T2N02: The Broken Gate [Climax; turn 4, way 1]
lines: T2
where: the mountain pass
who: Corra Dunmore (the scholar), Teague Barrowman (the knight), Rowan Hask (the thief)

At the mountain pass the dragon's wake is already sealing the gate, and the party must meet the dragon before the wake closes. You give Teague Barrowman the oath-blade back, Rowan Hask leads the party up the old road, and you make Corra Dunmore step aside before the name is spoken. The dragon is driven out, but its fire breaks the pass gate and leaves Corra Dunmore behind as the keeper who could not finish the rite.

### T2N03: The Open Road [Aftermath; ENDING]
lines: T2
where: the mountain pass
who: Corra Dunmore (the scholar), Teague Barrowman (the knight), Rowan Hask (the thief)

The road north is open, and you stand on the broken pass with Teague Barrowman and Rowan Hask while the dragon's smoke is still in the air. Corra Dunmore remains on the far side of the gate, holding the vial, no longer the scholar who traveled with you but the keeper the order sent.

who: Corra Dunmore (the scholar), Teague Barrowman (the knight), Rowan Hask (the thief)

Corra Dunmore sees you pry and says the rite is not for the party, but you, Rowan Hask, and Teague Barrowman agree to stand with her if the name must be answered.

lines: T3
where: the mountain pass
who: Corra Dunmore (the scholar), Teague Barrowman (the knight), Rowan Hask (the thief)

Before she speaks the name, you bind the shared ward with Rowan Hask's old road, Teague Barrowman's oath-blade, and your spent ward. The dragon sleeps, and the name takes its cost from the road rather than from Corra Dunmore's memory alone, so she still knows the party at the pass.
