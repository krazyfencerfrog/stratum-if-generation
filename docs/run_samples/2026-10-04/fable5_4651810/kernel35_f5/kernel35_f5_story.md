# kernel35_f5: story outline

Kernel: A ghost story that is funny and frightening in equal measure. You inherit a decrepit canal boat with a resident ghost who insists he is still the captain and will not let the boat leave its mooring. Your only living companion is your brother-in-law, who came to help you sell it and who wants his money back more than he wants you safe. I want the ghost to be a real character, scary when he needs to be, and I want the boat to actually go somewhere.

## Story form: Three-act structure + Episodic middle
The story begins with the boat stuck and the living owner's authority compromised, building through escalating, specific negotiations with the ghost as the sale deadline approaches. It should land on a decisive moment where the boat finally moves, resolving the tension between the dead captain's terms and the living owner's agency in a way that is both frightening and funny.
Why this form: The story follows a sustained trajectory of complications (the recurring instances of the ghost's conditions) that reverse at the midpoint (the midnight discovery) and bottom out in a crisis (the jammed lock gates) before settling in the decisive confrontation of the boat moving, which matches the 'want is disturbed... settled in one decisive confrontation' shape.

The question: Does the boat move by the dead captain's terms, or by the living owner's hand?

## Lines
### T1: The Salute at Dawn
- motivation: You start out wanting to prove you can command the boat and sell it before dawn, rather than be ruled by a dead man. By the end you want the boat to move under the captain it already has, even if that means your name is no longer the only one on it.
- strategy: Meet each of the ghost's nautical demands with a concrete living payment: answer his orders, pay with Lazlo's cash, and give his ledger a receipt for every fright.
- turning point: At midnight, the ghost points to the dark crack and Lazlo prices the fear. You realize the boat cannot be sold as a dead man's vessel unless you first let him name what is wrong with it.
- ending (N07) Under the Dead Captain's Name: The boat moves because you let Lake Kaur stand at the helm and name the course, while Lazlo pays for the fuel, the lock fee, and the lowered sale price. It has cost you full authority over the vessel and left you with a boat that is no longer entirely yours.

- strategy: Take the chain, keep the boat under your own hand, let Lazlo Brandt carry the buyer's mark and the cash, and get the boat sold and moving before the water can be saluted.
- turning point: The mooring chain key. Once you hold the key and Lazlo Brandt prices the scrape as a buyer's mark, the boat stops being something to answer the ghost and becomes something to sell under your own name.
