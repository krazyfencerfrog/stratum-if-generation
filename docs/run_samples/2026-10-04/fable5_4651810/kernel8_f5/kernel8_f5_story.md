# kernel8_f5: story outline

Kernel: Write me an interactive story. Surprise me.

## Story form: Kishotenketsu + Episodic middle
The story begins in a quiet, ordinary library and deepens as the room's strange logic reveals itself, moving from curiosity to a tense, surreal realization that the reader is being filed. It should land on a quiet, ambiguous reconciliation where the room's nature is accepted or resolved, rather than a dramatic confrontation or victory.
Why this form: The brief specifies no failure presence (3-0c.failure_presence) and a sustained trajectory (3b.primary_trajectory), which aligns with Kishotenketsu's lack of central conflict and its reliance on deepening situation. The 'Reconciliation' beat matches the required 'quiet, ambiguous resolution' (3c.moral_valence), whereas three_act and save_the_cat require climaxes or mirrored finales that contradict the brief's low-stakes, measured tone.

The question: Do you let the room finish its work, or do you stop it before it files you?

## Lines
### T1: The Reader in the Center
- motivation: You start out wanting to shelve the returned book and hand the reading room back to an ordinary nightly close, and by the end you want the people in the room to stay people, even if that means the room stops being what it was.
- strategy: You use the room's own record against itself: you check the page against the shelves, the crack, the lamp, and the chair, and you make Brisa Penhallow correct the fact she has been repeating because the record is all she has.
- turning point: When Brisa Penhallow's repeated correction turns out to be the record of a reader who has already been filed, you stop trying to make the room ordinary and start deciding who, if anyone, it will file.
- ending (N05) An Ordinary Close: The book is shelved, the central chair is out of the center, and the building closes as a plain municipal archive. It costs the room its purpose and the page that might have been you.
- world left: answer pole_b; standing: the night archivist, the night custodian; changed: the reading room is no longer a filing machine; the book is shelved and the nightly close is ordinary
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05

- strategy: Let Brisa Penhallow check the page’s added line against the shelves, the central table, and the crack, let her correct the page’s false line, and keep the corrected page at the center table so the room treats her as the reader it is filing while you hold the lamp and keep the key from Cadhla Marrack.
- turning point: The corrected page at the central table. Once Brisa Penhallow has checked the page against the room, the room no longer treats you as the only reader; it begins to pull her copy toward the center.

Her correction makes the page stop adding you for a moment, so you come to want the room to keep working under her correction rather than stopping it before it files you.
- differs: T1 stops the room before it files anyone and leaves both companions standing in an ordinary archive; this line lets the room finish its work by filing Brisa Penhallow, leaves Cadhla Marrack standing with his shift saved, and leaves the archive still working as a room that adds lines.

Cadhla Marrack saves his shift and leaves with the key, and the room’s purpose remains intact.
- world left: answer pole_a; standing: the night custodian; lost: the night archivist; changed: the central chair remains in the center occupied by Brisa Penhallow’s page; the room keeps adding lines instead of becoming an ordinary archive
- path: N01 > N02 > T2N01 > T2N02 > T2N03

### T3: The Locked Chair
- motivation: You start out wanting to shelve the returned book and finish the night, but once the shelves are locked and the crack has spelled READER, you come to want the key without giving the room your page, because the page is the only thing that can still name you as the reader; by the end you want the room stopped before it files you, even if stopping it means no one can open it again.

- turning point: The chair’s lock. When you move the chair out of the center, the room does not treat it as furniture but as the key’s housing: the chair stays locked in the corner, the shelves close, and the night key comes free only with the room’s mechanism half-stopped.

T2 lets the room finish by filing Brisa and keeps Cadhla’s working archive; this line stops the room before it files anyone and freezes the room itself, with Cadhla gone and the chair locked.

Cadhla Marrack leaves with the key, and the room becomes a sealed room that no one can open again.
- world left: answer pole_b; standing: the night archivist; lost: the night custodian; changed: the central chair remains locked in the corner and the shelves are closed for good, so the room is stopped but not ordinary
