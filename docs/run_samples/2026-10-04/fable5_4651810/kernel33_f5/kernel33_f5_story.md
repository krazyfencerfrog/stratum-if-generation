# kernel33_f5: story outline

Kernel: A slow-burn romance set over one summer at a failing seaside hotel. You're the new night manager; the other is the chef, who is about to leave for a better kitchen in the city. There's a rival for your attention and a reason the chef hasn't gone yet that nobody will say out loud. I want the pleasures of the genre: the near-misses, the one night everything almost happens, the moment one of you has to say it first. and at least one where it works out.

## Story form: Three-act structure + Countdown
The story begins with the arrival of a new manager and a chef who is quietly leaving, building through the summer's recurring near-misses and the deepening tension of the approaching city start date. It lands on a decisive moment of confession where the chef's hesitation is resolved, resulting in a tender commitment rather than a parting, matching the brief's transforming trajectory and best-case moral valence.
Why this form: The shape mirrors the engine's progression from the initial disturbance (the new date and the rival) through the escalating complications of the storm and the generator failure (midpoint/crisis) to the final confrontation in the lobby (climax) and the resolution of the relationship. This aligns with 3b.primary_trajectory's transforming arc and 3c.moral_valence's best case, whereas save_the_cat's 'All is lost' beat is too severe for the soft failure presence and kishotenketsu lacks the distinct climactic confrontation required by the engine's final turn.

The question: Do you say the swallowed sentence before the start date, or wait for the chef to say it before the summer ends?
