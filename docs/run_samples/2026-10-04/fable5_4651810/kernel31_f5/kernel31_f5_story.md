# kernel31_f5: story outline

Kernel: An epic fantasy adventure. You're a young hedge-witch from a border village who gets swept up in a quest to stop an old dragon that has woken in the northern mountains. Along the way you gather a small party — a disgraced knight, a thief who knows the old roads, and a scholar from the capital who has her own reasons for wanting to reach the dragon first. I want real travel: forests, a river city, ruins, the mountain pass. The companions should matter, with their own loyalties that can pull against yours. different endings depending on how you deal with the dragon and who is still with you at the end.

## Story form: Hero's journey (condensed)
The story begins with a young witch leaving a border village, driven by the escalating pressure of a waking dragon and the dispersing of her party. It builds through a sequence of escalating crises and relational fractures across dispersed locations, culminating in a confrontation where the protagonist must choose between duty and loyalty. The ending should feel like a decisive resolution of the threat, but the emotional weight lands on which bonds survived the journey, resulting in a best-case or worst-case outcome for the party's cohesion.
Why this form: Its shape matches the dispersed journey in 3f.setting_structure and the linear timeline in 3e.timeline_duration, moving from the 'Ordinary world' of the village to the 'Return' which aligns with the need to resolve the party's status (3c.moral_valence). While three_act is a fallback, the specific beats of leaving, trial, and return better scaffold the 'travel' and 'companion attachment' requirements (3b.secondary_affects) than the generic midpoint reversal of three_act, and fichtean_curve lacks the necessary setup for the initial departure and gathering of the party.

The question: What do you bring to the dragon's eye: the party's sword, the scholar's name, or your own hand on the old bond?

## Lines
### T1: The Smoke and the Stone
- motivation: You begin wanting to keep the party together long enough to stop the dragon, because a hedge-witch knows that a scattered party is already half defeated. By the end you want the dragon to sleep without making anyone in that party forget who they were to you, even if it costs the home you came from.
- strategy: Keep the three companions inside the same road, the same table, and the same gaze, and let the old debt be answered by the party’s bond rather than by one person’s silence.
- turning point: At the ruined waystation, Rozenn Dunmore shows the debt carved in her family’s hand but will not say who the name was paid for. From then on the quest is no longer only about reaching the dragon; it is about whether the party can stand in the dragon’s eye without being broken by the scholar’s need to pay alone.
- ending (N08) The Road Leaves the Village: The dragon sleeps because you press your own hand to the old bond in the lair floor and give the smoke of your village, while the party stands in the eye’s gaze together. It costs your hedgerows and the old road’s tie to your home: you can walk back, but you can no longer tend what you came from.
- world left: answer pole_b; standing: the disgraced knight, the thief, the scholar; lost: the village’s old road, the hedge-witch’s home-tending; changed: the dragon sleeps, the old road no longer reaches your village, and the party remains whole but changed by what was given at the eye
