# kernel32_f5: story outline

Kernel: A dungeon crawl where your only companion is a cursed talking sword that wants to be put back where it was found. Funny, but with teeth.

## Story form: Fichtean curve + In medias res
The story begins immediately in the dungeon, climbing through escalating hazards and the deepening bond with the sword until the final choice at the vault. It should land on the wry, bittersweet resolution of that choice, with a short aftermath that settles the emotional weight of the decision.
Why this form: Its shape matches the escalating trajectory (3b.primary_trajectory) and the short timeline (3e.timeline_duration), where the 'Opening crisis' is the first corridor, the middle beats are the guardian and rising water, and the 'Climax' is the vault choice, followed by a brief 'Aftermath' for the bittersweet restoration (3c.moral_valence).

The question: Do you set the blade into the black niche and leave the tower with an empty hand, or keep it in your hand and let the curse keep you alive?

## Lines
### T1: The Empty Hand
- motivation: You start out wanting to get out of the flooded tower alive with a steady hand and the only companion the dungeon has given you. By the end you want the sword to stop being a thing you carry and become a place it belongs, even if that leaves your hand empty.
- strategy: Use the sword as the tower’s temporary tool: let it point, press, cut, and tell its own story while you keep deferring the real choice until the niche is in front of you.
- turning point: In the dry chamber the sword finishes the last hand’s story and the skeleton’s grip stops looking like a warning and starts looking like a seat taken; from then on, every chance to set the blade down is no longer a delay but a test.
- ending (N05) The Empty Hand: You set the sword into the black niche and step back; the stone guardian lets the water stop and the tower sleep, and you leave with a steady, empty hand and no companion to complain about your grip.
- world left: answer pole_a; lost: the sword; changed: the tower is sealed and the water stills; the sword rests in the niche and your hand is empty
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > N05

### T2: The Marked Path
- motivation: You start out wanting to get out of the flooded tower alive with a steady hand and the only companion the dungeon has given you. After the far door narrows in answer to the groove you drag with the sword's pommel, you come to want to make the tower legible by leaving a path in its stones, because the tower reads the mark you leave. By the end you want the mark to be enough: a third answer that lets you leave with an empty hand without giving the sword back to the niche or keeping the curse in your grip.
- strategy: Keep using the sword as a surveyor's tool: drag its pommel to mark passages, answer the guardian's narrowing, and at the vault set the blade on the floor as a marker between you and the niche rather than into the niche or back in your hand.
- turning point: At the vault threshold you realize the guardian is not waiting for you to choose the sword; it is waiting for the tower to be answered by a mark, so you stop treating the blade as either yours or the niche's and start using it as the tower's own sign.
- leaves T1 after N01 when: you dragged the sword's pommel across the pressure plates to mark a path and stepped on them as they rose
  - instead of: you let the sword lay flat on the nearest plate and followed the dry line of stone it pointed to
  - why the shift: The far door narrows a hand's width after you drag the pommel across the plates, and the sword complains that you are marking its way through its own house. You learn that the tower reads the mark you leave, and the blade becomes a surveyor's tool: you come to want to make the tower legible by leaving a path in its stones, because the narrowing shows you the tower can be answered by a mark, not only by returning the blade.
- differs: T1 answers the story's question by putting the sword into the black niche and letting the tower sleep with an empty hand, while this line answers with neither: the sword is left as a mark in the tower's floor, the niche stays open, and the water keeps moving.
- ending (T2N04) The Marked Path: You drag the sword's pommel across the vault floor and set the blade in the groove between you and the black niche, neither returned nor kept. The stone guardian follows the mark and lets you pass with an empty hand, but the niche stays open and the water keeps moving because the blade is not home. You leave the tower with a steady hand and no companion, and the tower is left awake with a path written in its stones.
- world left: answer neither; lost: the sword; changed: the tower has a marked path and the niche remains open, so the water is still moving
- path: N01 > T2N01 > T2N02 > T2N03 > T2N04

### T3: The One Who Kept It
- motivation: You start out wanting to get out of the flooded tower alive with a steady hand and the only companion the dungeon has given you. At the vault the sword's warmth in your palm becomes the only proof that you were allowed to keep anything, and you come to want to keep the blade because the guardian's closing door is a taking, not an invitation; by the end you want to be the keeper, even if your hand shakes and the tower stays awake.
- strategy: Refuse the niche, cut the guardian's stone door open with the sword, and leave with the blade in your hand, accepting the curse's weight and the guardian's mark.
- turning point: The guardian's hand closes toward the vault while the sword grows heavier in your palm: the niche stops being an exit and becomes a taking, so you cut the stone door open instead of offering the blade.
- leaves T1 after N04 when: you refused the niche and cut the guardian's stone door open
  - instead of: you set the blade into the black stone and stepped back
  - why the shift: The sword grows heavier in your hand and the niche pulls at your wrist while the curse warms your palm, and the guardian's closing door does not ask you to offer the blade: it closes as if to take it. You learn that keeping the sword is the only way to stop the tower from deciding what you are allowed to hold.
- differs: Compared with T1, this line gives the other answer to the question: the sword stays in your shaking hand and the tower stays awake rather than sleeping with the niche closed and your hand empty. Compared with T2, this line does not make a third mark in the stones or leave the sword on the floor; it keeps the blade as a companion, so the price is the curse in your arm rather than the sword's absence.
- ending (T3N01) The One Who Kept It: You leave the tower with the sword in your shaking hand while the curse takes weight in your arm. The guardian marks you as the one who kept it, the niche remains open, and the water rises through the lower rooms.
- world left: answer pole_b; standing: the sword; changed: the niche remains open and the guardian marks you as the one who kept the sword, so the tower is awake
- path: N01 > N02 > N03 > N04 > T3N01

## Nodes
### N01: The First Crossing [Opening crisis; turn 1, way 1; event 1]
lines: T1, T2, T3
where: the first corridor
who: the sword, the stone guardian

The first corridor's pressure plates start dropping the stone door behind you, and you let the sword lie flat on the nearest plate while it counts your steps and criticizes your grip like a commuter dictating parade order. You follow the dry line of stone it points to while the door drops to your shoulders and the stone guardian's hand closes the far door. You cross with the sword still in your hand, and the tower has shown you that the blade can be set down as a tool without being returned.

Image: the sword flat on a pressure plate, the door dropping

### N02: The Last Hand [Second crisis; turn 2, way 1; event 2]
lines: T1, T3
where: the dry chamber
who: the sword, the stone guardian, the skeleton in the wall

In the dry chamber, the stone guardian seals the stair behind you and leaves only a crack in the wall, while the sword tells the story of the last hand that held it without being asked and the wall shows the skeleton still gripping the blade. You let it finish before the crack closes, and it ends by asking whether you have a map of the tower and whether you accept apologies at the exit. The chamber goes quiet, and the grip looks less like a warning and more like a seat taken, so the sword is no longer just a weapon but a history you are holding.

Image: a skeleton's hand still closed around the blade in the wall

### N03: The Threshold [Third crisis; turn 3, way 1; event 3]
lines: T1, T3
where: the vault
who: the sword, the stone guardian

At the vault, the black niche opens and the stone guardian wakes, and the sword grows heavier in your hand as you hesitate to put it back. You tell the guardian what the last hand did and set the sword's pommel on the threshold while the water takes the bottom of the stair. The guardian lifts the vault door only enough for you to pass while the sword is out of your hand for the first time, and you enter with the blade still yours, but you have had to let it rest on the threshold, and the tower now knows you can do it.

Image: the sword's pommel resting on the vault threshold

### N04: The Return to the Niche [Climax; turn 4, way 1]
lines: T1, T3
where: the vault
who: the sword, the stone guardian

In the vault the black niche waits while the sword grows heavier in your hand, warming your palm into a perfect fit at the worst moment. The stone guardian does not negotiate, only closes, and the sword makes one last commuter's complaint about the fit as the water presses against the lower stones. You set the blade into the black stone and step back, and the niche closes around it, the guardian's eyes drop, and the water stops rising as your hand goes empty.

Image: the black niche closing over the sword, the waterline stopped

### N05: The Empty Hand [Aftermath; ENDING]
lines: T1
where: the vault, the broken stair
who: the sword, the stone guardian

You take the broken stair with an empty hand and whatever you saved, your grip steady now that the curse is no longer in your arm. In the vault behind you, the stone guardian lets the tower sleep and the water stand still, and the sword, back in the black stone, settles into its place without another complaint. What remains is the quiet cost: the tower is safe, the sword is home, and your hand is empty.

Image: your steady, empty hand at the broken stair

### T2N01: The Last Hand's Mark [Second crisis; turn 2, way 2; event 2]
lines: T2
where: the dry chamber
who: the sword, the stone guardian, the skeleton in the wall

In the dry chamber the stone guardian seals the stair behind you and leaves only a crack in the wall. The sword starts telling the last hand's story before you can stop it, in the tone of a commuter interrupted while reading a parking ticket, and you set its pommel on the flagstone to read the dragged groove beside the skeleton in the wall, whose hand still grips the stone. The stone guardian grinds the stair shut while the sword complains that you have the manners of a thief, leaving you with the mark and the blade as a stylus.

Image: the dragged groove beside the skeleton, still dark in the wall

### T2N02: The Latch Cut [Third crisis; turn 3, way 2; event 3]
lines: T2
where: the vault
who: the sword, the stone guardian

At the vault the black niche opens and the stone guardian wakes, its slow eyes tracking the sword and the mark you have left behind. The water has risen to the bottom of the broken stair, and the sword grows heavier in your hand as you hesitate to put it back, so you drag the pommel along the guardian's stone latch and score a line that makes the narrowing look like a question. The vault opens because the mark is an answer the tower can follow, but the blade is now part of the path you are making as it complains that you are turning its house into a signpost.

Image: the scored line along the stone latch, still dark

### T2N03: The Groove [Climax; turn 4]
lines: T2
where: the vault
who: the sword, the stone guardian

In the vault the black niche waits and the sword grows heavier in your hand as you hesitate, the curse warming your palm and making it fit perfectly at the worst possible moment. Instead of setting the blade into the niche or refusing it in your hand, you drag the pommel across the vault floor from the threshold to the niche and set the sword in the groove between you and the black stone, pommel toward you and tip toward the niche but not in it. The stone guardian follows the mark, its hand sliding along the groove and opening the vault door only along the line you have made, wide enough for you but not for the blade, while the water keeps moving and the curse leaves your hand but not the tower.

Image: the sword in the groove, pommel toward you, tip toward the niche

### T2N04: The Marked Exit [Aftermath; ENDING]
lines: T2
where: the vault, the broken stair
who: the sword, the stone guardian

You leave the tower with an empty hand and whatever you saved, your grip steady now that the sword is no longer in your arm. The stone guardian remains at the vault, the door still open only along your line, while the black niche stays open and the sword lies in the groove you dragged across the floor. The water keeps moving through the lower rooms because the blade is not home, and the only companion the dungeon has let you keep is now a mark in the tower's stones.

Image: the sound of water moving through the lower rooms under your feet

### T3N01: The Keeper's Mark [Aftermath; ENDING]
lines: T3
where: the vault, the broken stair
who: the sword, the stone guardian

The guardian's hand closes the vault door behind you and leaves a cold stone seam across your wrist, marking you as the one who kept the blade. The water in the lower rooms keeps rising because the black niche is still open, and the sword, still in your shaking hand, calls the mark a permanent employee badge and complains that its return policy is void. You leave the tower by the broken stair with the sword in your hand while the curse takes weight in your arm.

Image: the cold stone seam across your wrist, sword still in hand
