# kernel34_f5: story outline

Kernel: Court intrigue. You're the tutor to a child king whose uncle, the regent, means to crown himself at the midsummer rite in nine days. The regent is clever, patient, and genuinely believes the boy is unfit. You have no soldiers: only what the child trusts you with, what the court's servants let slip, and whichever of three courtiers you can bring over. The regent's plan should advance on its own whether or not I do anything about it, and I want to be able to lose people I've won.

## Story form: Fichtean curve + Countdown
The story begins with the immediate, hard threat of the regent's decree and escalates through a series of distinct crises—each turning or loss of an ally—driven by the relentless nine-day clock. It lands on the final confrontation at the rite, where the child's trust is tested against the sealed oath, leaving the outcome dependent on the specific choices made in the preceding days.
Why this form: The brief's escalating trajectory (3b.primary_trajectory) and hard failure presence (3-0c.failure_presence) align with Fichtean's structure of climbing through crises to a climax. The specific engine turns (decree, removal, seating, sealing, rite) map directly to the opening and subsequent crises, leading to the climax at the rite, which matches the linear timeline (3e.timeline_structure) and the need for a decisive end point rather than an unwinding.

The question: What is the child's trust for: a crown he can keep, or a crown that keeps him safe?

## Lines
### T1: The Child's Hand
- motivation: You begin by wanting to keep Wren Tollen safe and the crown in his hand by the midsummer rite. By the end you want him to answer for himself, even if answering costs you the trust he has been giving you in small, frightened questions.
- strategy: Use the servants' information to expose the regent's seating and oath before the rite is sealed, and bring the treasury courtier and the oath courtier to stand with the child by giving each a reason that is not fear.
- turning point: When Linden Dunmore fixes the child's seat beside Morrow Ludd rather than before the crown, you realize the rite is not preparing the child to rule; it is moving him out of the way. You trade Wren's private fear to Rurik Merrow to change the seating, and from then on the child's trust is a thing you spend rather than a thing you protect.
- ending (N06) A Crown Answered: The child's hand goes on the crown because Rurik Merrow and Brannoc Glaive stand in the rite chamber and Wren Tollen answers the first question in the old form.
