# kernel38: story outline

Kernel: A Victorian country-house murder. A snowstorm has cut the house off for two days, and the master of the house is dead in his locked study. You're the new housekeeper, the only person who saw where everyone was that night. Seven guests, all lying about something. I want real clues and red herrings, a fair solution, and I want to be able to accuse the wrong person.

## Story form: Seven-point structure
The story begins in the immediate aftermath of the murder, where the protagonist is thrust into the role of investigator, and escalates through the night as alibis crumble and the storm tightens the trap. It lands on a single, decisive moment of accusation in the drawing room before dawn, where the truth is revealed or the protagonist fails, ending the suspense with a hard, irreversible consequence.
Why this form: Its structure builds from a hook (the discovery) to a resolution (the accusation) via a midpoint where the protagonist stops reacting and starts choosing, which matches the engine's single_fork decision mechanism and the hard failure cost of a wrong answer. The two pinches correspond to the escalating pressure of the storm and the failing alibis described in the brief's timeline and failure triggers.

## You: Philippa Montague
- who: You are the newly hired housekeeper of a snowbound Victorian country house, and the only person who saw where each guest was during the night in which the master died.
- history: You arrived two days before the storm to replace a housekeeper the master dismissed in a quarrel, and you were given the house keys, the watch log, and the duty of checking the rooms each hour.
- wants: to name the murderer before the storm breaks and the guests can leave.
- need: to decide whether your watch log is worth more than the guests’ polished stories, and whether you can trust what you saw.
- left to the player: whether you accuse the person your watch log points to, or the person the guests want you to point at.
- tie, the master’s business partner: the guest who signed the master’s accounts and who watched you take the watch log as if it were a threat
- tie, the guest with the bandaged hand: the guest the others keep pointing at when the weapon is mentioned, and who flinches when you enter a room
- tie, the master’s timid niece: the guest who has seen something she will not say until the accusation is made

The question: Do you accuse the guest whose alibi your watch log breaks, or the guest the house wants you to name?
