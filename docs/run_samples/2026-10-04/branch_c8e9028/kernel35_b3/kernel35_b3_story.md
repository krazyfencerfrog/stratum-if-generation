# kernel35_b3: story outline

Kernel: A ghost story that is funny and frightening in equal measure. You inherit a decrepit canal boat with a resident ghost who insists he is still the captain and will not let the boat leave its mooring. Your only living companion is your brother-in-law, who came to help you sell it and who wants his money back more than he wants you safe. I want the ghost to be a real character, scary when he needs to be, and I want the boat to actually go somewhere.

## Story form: Kishotenketsu + Episodic middle
The story opens with the boat stuck and the living wanting to sell it, then escalates through escalating, self-contained hauntings and bargaining attempts that deepen the absurdity rather than the danger. It should land on the boat actually moving, not because the ghost is defeated, but because the situation resolves into a comic, uneasy success where the dead captain's authority is acknowledged in a way that lets the living move forward.
Why this form: Its four-beat structure matches the brief's 'sustained' trajectory (3b.primary_trajectory) and 'none' failure presence (3-0c.failure_presence), where the conflict is a negotiation rather than a hard defeat. The 'Turn' (ten) aligns with the engine's 'confront' turn where the line snaps, and the 'Reconciliation' (ketsu) matches the 'comic, uneasy success' (3c.moral_valence) where the boat moves and the brother-in-law's money is addressed, rather than a climactic battle.

## You: Ingrid Kowalczyk
- who: You are the new owner of a narrow, leaking narrowboat, the only living person aboard while a dead captain still keeps the log.
- history: You inherited the boat from your late sister, who kept it as a summer house and left you the keys, the unpaid canal dues, and a brother-in-law who paid to clear the title and now wants that money back.
- wants: to get the boat under way and to a place where it can be sold before your brother-in-law's patience runs out.
- need: to find out whether owning a boat means you can move it, or whether you must answer to everyone who ever kept it.
- left to the player: whether you learn to command the boat as a living owner or let the dead captain's protocol decide your terms.
- tie, your brother-in-law: your sister's husband, the man who paid to clear the title and now counts every coin as if your safety were a line item.
- tie, the ghost captain: the dead man in the rotting uniform who still checks the logs and complains about the bilge water as if you were both still on duty.

The question: Who has the right to make this boat move: the dead captain who keeps the log, or the living owner who holds the key?
