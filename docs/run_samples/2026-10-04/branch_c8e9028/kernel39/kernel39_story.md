# kernel39: story outline

Kernel: Ancient Rome, the year of the four emperors. You're a freedwoman who runs a bathhouse where senators talk too freely, and every new emperor wants to know what you overheard under the last one. I want politics, survival, and family, and I want it to feel like real history, not a toga movie.

## Story form: Fichtean curve + In medias res
The story opens with the immediate threat of the first emperor's death and the arrival of the Praetorian officer, establishing a pattern of recurring crises that escalate with each succession. It builds through a series of high-stakes interrogations and betrayals, where the cost of survival rises from political favor to family safety, landing on a final confrontation where the protagonist must choose between honest testimony and the selective silence required to keep her daughter alive.
Why this form: Its shape opens inside the first crisis (the officer's arrival in Turn 1) and climbs through distinct crises of different kinds (the trade in Turn 2, the rescue/family threat in Turn 3) to a climax, which matches the escalating trajectory in 3b.primary_trajectory and the recurring instances in 3-0a.decision_mechanism. Unlike three_act, which requires a distinct setup before the inciting incident, or seven_point, which relies on a midpoint shift in agency, fichtean_curve's continuous crisis-driven structure better fits the 'year of the four emperors' premise where the threat is constant and the stakes escalate without a lull.

## You: Flavia Crassa
- who: You are a freedwoman who owns a working bathhouse in a Roman city district, where senators come to be scrubbed and speak too freely.
- history: You were freed by a senator's steward after years as a bathhouse attendant, and you bought the caldarium with a dowry and a debt to the imperial oil merchant.
- wants: to keep your daughter safe and the bathhouse open through the next succession without selling your memory for a price that breaks your family
- need: to find out whether the truth you kept can still be trusted to keep you alive
- left to the player: whether you become the honest keeper of the truth or the mother who buys safety with a name
- tie, the Praetorian officer: the man who has come to your bathhouse at each succession and offers a price for your silence
- tie, the rival freedman: the owner of the bathhouse across the street who also listens and sells what he hears
- tie, the tired senator: the man who said the wrong thing in the caldarium and whose fate hangs on your memory
- tie, your daughter: the scribe in the imperial chancery who copies the purge lists

The question: What do you hand to the new emperor: the honest testimony you kept, or the selective silence that keeps your family alive?
