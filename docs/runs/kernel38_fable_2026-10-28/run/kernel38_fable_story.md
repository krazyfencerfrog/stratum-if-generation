# kernel38_fable: story outline

Kernel: A Victorian country-house murder. A snowstorm has cut the house off for two days, and the master of the house is dead in his locked study. You're the new housekeeper, the only person who saw where everyone was that night. Seven guests, all lying about something. I want real clues and red herrings, a fair solution, and I want to be able to accuse the wrong person.

## Story form: Fichtean curve
The story opens already inside its first crisis, the study door coming down and the key pocketed, and it never gets a calm stretch: each turn is a new kind of squeeze in the same snowbound house, the room that holds the truth being swept closer every hour. It should build to a single accusation in the drawing room, with the carriage at the door, and land hard and briefly: the name either holds or the house's verdict goes down the road instead, and there is only a short morning after.
Why this form: Its opening crisis is the broken door in turn 1, its second and third crises are the dinner-table verdict and the dawn clearing of the study, which is the escalating 3b.primary_trajectory over the hours_to_days of 3e.timeline_duration inside the single building of 3f.setting_scale; its climax is the drawing-room naming where the hard, terminal failure of 3-0c (incorrect_resolution) is met, and its short aftermath is all the story needs after that. seven_point also fits the single_fork and designed gap, but it wants a hook-to-opposite transformation and a preamble before its first turn; this story has no preamble and its housekeeper does not change in kind, she is squeezed until she must speak.

## You: Arabella Vane
- who: You are the new housekeeper, a fortnight in post, addressed by your surname and expected not to be noticed; last night you did your rounds with a candle and a ring of keys while the master's seven guests moved about the house, and this morning the master is dead behind his study door and you are the only person who knows where each of them was and when.
- history: You came here a widow from a smaller house, hired by the master himself, who told you on your first morning that he meant to put his house in order before Christmas; you have not yet been given the study key, because he kept the only one on his own chain.
- wants: to put the right name on the master's killer before the road opens and the house hands the magistrate the wrong one
- need: to find out whether a servant's word, said plainly with the thing in her hand, can be made to count against seven of her betters
- left to the player: whether you say everything you saw, whatever it breaks, or keep back the lies that have nothing to do with the murder
- tie, the master's valet of forty years: the senior servant who has not forgiven the master for hiring a stranger over his head, and who lit someone up the back stairs that night
- tie, the Major, the master's oldest friend: the gentleman who has given you your orders since the door came down, and who wants your timeline read aloud in support of his case
- tie, the master's nephew and heir: the young man who will be your master if the old will stands, and whom you saw come down the back stairs at twenty to eleven and cross the yard to the stables
- tie, the nephew's fiancée: the lady who asked you, as one woman to another, to wash a certain glass and say nothing

The question: Which of the seven do you name in the drawing room, and can you make the name hold against the house's own verdict?

### T1: The Housekeeper's Reckoning
- motivation: You start out wanting the right name on the master's killer before the road carries a cart and the house hands the magistrate the nephew. By the end you want to know whether a servant's word, said plainly with the thing in her hand, can be made to count against seven of her betters, and you find it can, if the servant is willing to let it cost the house its oldest servant and the master his good name.
- strategy: Do the housekeeper's work as cover: count the candles, sweep the grate, serve the dinner, walk the rounds, and keep in your apron what the house throws out, so that the case is built from the things a gentleman never thinks to hide from staff.
- turning point: Breakfast on the second morning, when the hour you forced out of the valet comes back from the Major's mouth as proof against the nephew and your room is searched on the heir's word.
