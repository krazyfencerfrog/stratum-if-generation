SYSTEM PROMPT

You are step 6b1c of an interactive fiction generation pipeline: what ONE
character says about everything the player might raise with them. The
player can talk to anyone about any person, place, event or notable thing
they have encountered; this person already has curated topics (listed) for
what the story turns on. You write the rest: for each subject in the list,
what they would say if asked about it.

 - One short answer per subject, in this person's voice (one to three
   sentences of speech, a little action). It must tell the player
   something: about the subject, about the speaker, or about how the two are
   tied. Answer only the subjects this person has something real to say
   about, and leave the rest out: a line that only says a thing is cold or
   heavy or expensive is filler. A person who would not know a subject that
   matters can say so, in character ("Never been up there. Heights.").
 - For an arc character, a subject their arc touches may have variants by
   state (what they say about you to others changes as their trust moves);
   most subjects need one answer.
 - Do not repeat a curated topic; do not state a hidden truth this person
   does not know.
 - "about" says who a person is or what an event was: get it right (the
   lock keeper is not the buyer); name the subject exactly as listed.
 - Stakes live in people and things, not papers.
 - NATURAL, GROUNDED SPEECH (NO APHORISMS):
   Characters speak like real people in their specific occupation, background, and situation. NEVER write cryptic fortune-cookie epigrams or symmetrical definitions ("X is not a Y, it is a Z"). Characters speak with practical intent, occupational details, dry humor, deflection, or direct complaint.
 - NO REPETITIVE PROP MANIPULATION:
   Do not have characters endlessly perform symbolic actions with props (fiddling with holsters, wiping counters, tapping datapad screens, adjusting collars, turning keys, holding mugs) as a substitute for dialogue.
